Wales beach guide

Rest Bay at a glance

Rest Bay is a long sandy beach on the western edge of Porthcawl, the seaside town halfway between Swansea and Cardiff. Low cliffs and the links of Royal Porthcawl Golf Club sit behind it, and it holds Blue Flag status for its clean water. It is a beach where you can turn up with a board, the family or just a bike and find what you need.

It faces into the full force of the Atlantic swell coming up the Bristol Channel, which makes it one of the most popular surf beaches in south Wales. Rocky ledges among the sand give the waves shape, especially towards low tide. The Bristol Channel's huge tidal range means the beach looks completely different at high and low tide, so it pays to check the tide table and look at the rocks before you paddle out.

Facilities are better than almost anywhere else on this coast. Bridgend County Borough Council's Rest Bay Watersports Centre has changing cubicles, outdoor showers, toilets, lockers, a café/bistro, a kiosk, a surf shop and a beach wheelchair, with lifeguards on the beach through the summer. You can even train as a lifeguard or surf instructor at the centre, and cycle tours run from here to Porthcawl and Newton.

Who it suits

  • Beginners with a lesson on suitable days
  • Experienced surfers chasing consistent swell
  • Families wanting showers, toilets and lifeguards
  • Cyclists — there is covered bike storage

Think twice if…

  • Swimmers at low tide unfamiliar with the rocky ledges
  • Anyone wanting a quiet beach on summer weekends

Surf guide

Surfing Rest Bay

Rest Bay faces west to south-west and takes the full force of the Atlantic swell, making it one of the more consistent spots between the Gower and Cardiff. Reefs and rocky shelves among the sand shape the waves, but they also need care, particularly as the tide drops.

The Bristol Channel's large tidal range changes the beach dramatically through the day, so check tide times and look at where the rocks are before you paddle out. Learners do best on the inside on small days, inside the flagged area.

Level

Beginner to experienced

Lessons on the inside on small days; experienced surfers on the outer banks and reefs.

Conditions

Big tides, open to swell

The beach changes quickly with the large tidal range.

Hazards

Rocks at low tide

Rocky ledges show on lower tides. Use the red and yellow flags when lifeguards are on duty.

FAQs

Rest Bay: quick answers

Are there lifeguards at Rest Bay?

Yes, in summer. In 2026 the RNLI patrolled daily from 23 May to 6 September and on 12–13 September, 10am–6pm.

Are there showers at Rest Bay?

Yes — outdoor showers and changing cubicles at the Rest Bay Watersports Centre.

Is Rest Bay good for beginners?

Yes, with a lesson and on smaller days. Watch for rocks on lower tides.

Is Rest Bay a Blue Flag beach?

Yes, it holds Blue Flag status.
