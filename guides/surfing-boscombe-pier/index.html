Southern England beach guide

Boscombe Pier at a glance

Boscombe Pier sits roughly in the middle of Bournemouth's long run of sand, at the foot of Boscombe Chine. The pier first opened in 1889, was partly taken down in the Second World War to deter invasion, and reopened after restoration in 2008. Its entrance building is Grade II listed. Either side of it you'll find a broad sandy beach with views right across Poole Bay, backed by the promenade and the cliffs.

This is the most practical stretch of Bournemouth seafront for a surf day. The beach has surf equipment hire, showers, drinking water, toilets and a beach office, plus a pub, a diner, restaurants and a kiosk on the promenade. Boscombe Undercliff car park sits right next to the beach with level access, and Pokesdown station is just over a mile away, so you can easily come without a car.

Don't come expecting the artificial reef to help. Built off the pier in 2009 as Europe's first artificial surf reef, it fell short of its performance targets, was closed for safety reasons in 2011 and has since been rebranded as a coastal activity park. Waves here depend on swell pushing up the Channel into Poole Bay, so most days are small and plenty are flat.

Who it suits

  • First surf lessons on small, clean days
  • Families wanting lifeguards and facilities close together
  • Car-free trips via Pokesdown or Bournemouth stations
  • Beginners who want hire and showers on the spot

Think twice if…

  • Anyone wanting reliable or powerful surf
  • Dog walkers on this beach between May and September

Surf guide

Surfing Boscombe Pier

Boscombe is a sandy beach break inside the shelter of Poole Bay. It needs a decent south-westerly swell travelling up the Channel to produce rideable waves, and northerly winds are offshore. Outside of those days the water is often flat, which suits paddleboarding more than surfing.

The pier and the groynes along this stretch can concentrate currents and are hard objects to avoid, so keep a good distance and stay between the red and yellow flags when the RNLI are on duty. The old reef area offshore is not a dependable wave.

Level

Beginners to improvers

Small, gentle waves when there is swell; best with an instructor if you're new.

Hazards

Pier legs and groynes

Keep well clear of the pier and groynes and follow the lifeguards' flags.

Season

Autumn and winter

Channel swell is most likely from autumn to early spring; summer is usually small and much busier.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Lifeguards

RNLI, two patrols

In 2026 Boscombe East was patrolled 28 March–27 September and Boscombe West 23 May–6 September, daily 10am–6pm.

Parking

Undercliff and Overstrand

Boscombe Undercliff car park sits beside the beach with level access; Overstrand car park is the other nearby option.

Accessibility

Matting and a beach wheelchair

Mobi-Mat access matting is laid May–September and a beach wheelchair can be borrowed from the beach office in season. Accessible toilets are at the Overstrand and near the Prom Diner.

Dogs

Summer restrictions

From 1 May to 30 September dogs are banned from BCP's Blue Flag and Seaside Award beaches; leads are required on the promenade and zig-zags all year. Check the council's dog map for nearby year-round stretches.

Getting here

Rail and bus

Pokesdown station is about 1.2 miles away and Bournemouth station about 1.8 miles; morebus services run to Boscombe.

FAQs

Boscombe Pier: quick answers

Does the Boscombe surf reef work?

No. It was finished in 2009 but didn't meet its performance targets, closed for safety reasons in 2011 and is now branded a coastal activity park. Surf here depends on Channel swell.

Are there lifeguards at Boscombe?

Yes. In 2026 Boscombe East was patrolled 28 March–27 September and Boscombe West 23 May–6 September, 10am–6pm.

Where do you park for Boscombe Pier?

Boscombe Undercliff car park is right by the beach with level access; the Overstrand car park is also close.

Can you get to Boscombe beach by train?

Yes. Pokesdown is about 1.2 miles away and Bournemouth station about 1.8 miles, with buses to the seafront.
