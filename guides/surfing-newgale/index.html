Wales beach guide

Newgale at a glance

Newgale is the long beach you see from the A487 as it drops down to sea level between Haverfordwest and St Davids. Nearly two miles of flat sand face west into St Brides Bay, and a high bank of rounded pebbles — thrown up by a great storm in October 1859 — separates the beach from the road, a pub, a surf shop and a scatter of campsites.

Because it faces straight out to the Atlantic, Newgale usually has more swell than the more sheltered beaches further round the bay, which makes it the default surf check for this part of Pembrokeshire. The flip side is wind: on a westerly day it is blown out, while on a calm or easterly day it can be very good.

There is very little village — the Duke of Edinburgh Inn, Newsurf's beachfront hire shop, a café, toilets and parking — so most people treat Newgale as a beach day and stay in Solva, St Davids, Broad Haven or one of the holiday parks nearby.

Who it suits

  • Surfers who want the most consistent beach in St Brides Bay
  • Beginners on small days, ideally with lessons or between the flags
  • Families who like a huge beach and simple, close-to-the-car facilities
  • Kitesurfers and windsurfers when the wind is right

Think twice if…

  • Sheltered paddling on a big swell day — Broad Haven or Nolton Haven are calmer
  • Anyone wanting a village with shops and restaurants on the doorstep

Surf guide

Surfing Newgale

A straightforward, exposed beach break that picks up almost any west or south-west swell.

Newgale faces almost due west, so it sees more of the Atlantic than any other beach in St Brides Bay. Sandbanks shift along the length of the beach; the waves are usually better defined on the lower half of the tide, and the shore break against the pebbles can be heavy at high tide.

It works best with light winds from the east or north-east. Anything with west in it quickly makes the surface choppy, which is why local surfers often move to more sheltered spots when the wind is onshore.

Level

Small days for learners, bigger days for improvers

On a small, clean day the inside whitewater is a good learning environment. When the swell builds, rips form between the banks and it becomes a beach for competent surfers only.

Conditions

West to south-west swell, easterly wind

Exposed to most swell. Offshore winds are from the east. Check a beach-level forecast and the tide before you go.

Hazards

Rips, wind and a steep pebble bank

Rip currents form between sandbanks on bigger days. Strong onshore wind is common. The pebble bank is steep — take care carrying boards.

Other sports

Kite and windsurfing

The open beach is popular with kitesurfers, windsurfers and power-kiters. Give each other space and keep away from the bathing area.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Three car parks behind the beach

There are three car parks alongside the beach, with disabled parking in the main car park. Charges and payment methods change — check the signs when you arrive. Postcode SA62 6AS.

Toilets

Toilets in the middle car park

Public toilets, including accessible facilities, are in the middle car park area. Hot showers are offered at Newsurf.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI cover

RNLI lifeguards patrol a section of Newgale during the summer season. Swim and bodyboard between the red and yellow flags; surf between the black and white flags.

Dogs

Seasonal ban on the central third

Dogs are not allowed on the central third of the beach from 1 May to 30 September. The northern and southern ends are dog-friendly all year — follow the signs on the beach.

Access

Over the pebble bank

Reaching the sand means crossing the pebble bank, which is steep in places. The slipway area by the main car park is the easiest route with a pushchair or heavy kit.

Safety

Tides at the north end

At low tide you can walk north towards Pwll March, but the tide cuts this route off. Check tide times and allow plenty of time to get back.

Accommodation

Where to stay near Newgale

Newgale itself is mostly campsites and holiday lets. For more choice, look at Solva, St Davids or Broad Haven.

On the beach

Newgale campsites and lets

Camping and caravan sites sit right behind the pebble bank. Wikipedia notes that pitches on the lowest ground can flood in heavy summer rain, so ask which pitch you are getting.

Village base

Solva

A small harbour village about 3 miles west with pubs, cafés and galleries. A good base for couples who want evenings out without driving far.

City base

St Davids

Britain's smallest city, about 7 miles west. Hotels, B&Bs and restaurants, and close to Whitesands for a second surf option.

Family base

Broad Haven

About 7 miles south by road. A gentler, more sheltered beach with shops and cafés — handy when Newgale is too big.

Beyond the surf

Things to do around Newgale

Walk

Pembrokeshire Coast Path

The National Trail runs right past Newgale. Walk north over the headland towards Solva, or south to Nolton Haven.

Village

Solva

A sheltered harbour inlet with galleries and cafés, and a short climb to the Gribin headland for views.

History

St Davids Cathedral

The medieval cathedral and the ruins of the Bishop's Palace are about 7 miles west.

Did you know?

The Landsker line

The Brandy Brook, which reaches the sea at Newgale, is traditionally seen as the line between Welsh-speaking north Pembrokeshire and English-speaking south Pembrokeshire.

Travel

Getting to Newgale

By car

On the A487

Newgale is on the A487 between Haverfordwest (about 8 miles) and St Davids. The road runs right along the back of the beach and can close in severe storms; Pembrokeshire County Council is developing a scheme to move it inland.

By bus

TrawsCymru T11

The T11 between Haverfordwest and Fishguard via St Davids stops at Newgale. Check current timetables on Traveline Cymru.

By train

Haverfordwest

The nearest railway station is Haverfordwest, then the T11 bus or a taxi.

FAQs

Newgale: quick answers

Is Newgale good for beginners?

On small, clean days the whitewater close to shore is good for learning, and lifeguards patrol in summer. When the swell is bigger, rips form and it is better suited to experienced surfers — take a lesson or choose a calmer beach such as Broad Haven.

Can I take my dog to Newgale?

Yes, but not on the central third of the beach between 1 May and 30 September. The north and south sections are dog-friendly all year.

Where do I park at Newgale?

There are three car parks beside the beach on the A487, with disabled parking in the main car park. Check the signs for current charges.

Is there surf hire at Newgale?

Yes. Newsurf, beside the Duke of Edinburgh pub, hires surfboards, bodyboards, wetsuits, kayaks and paddleboards.

When is Newgale at its best for surfing?

With a west or south-west swell and light easterly winds. Westerly winds blow straight onshore and make it messy.
