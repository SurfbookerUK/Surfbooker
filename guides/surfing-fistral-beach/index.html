Cornwall beach guide

Fistral at a glance

Fistral is a straight sandy beach about 750 metres long, half a mile west of Newquay town centre. It fills Fistral Bay between Towan Head to the north and Pentire Point East to the south, faces west-northwest into the Atlantic and is backed by steep dunes. The Headland Hotel, built in 1897, looks down from Towan Head, and the name is thought to come from a Cornish word for foul water.

It's the home of British surfing in many ways. Major international contests have been held here, and the British Surfing Association, Newquay Surf Life Saving Club and Newquay Boardriders are all based at the beach. North Fistral has a competition building and a surf museum, and surf shops, hire, toilets and food cluster around the beach complex. On summer days and event weekends, expect a crowd in the water and on the sand.

Fistral is exposed, which is why it's so consistent and also why it needs respect. The north end is a challenging stretch of water, while the south end, tucked behind Pentire, is usually mellower. Rips run along the rocks, and the RNLI has reported large rescues from rip currents here. The RNLI patrols for a long season, so surf and swim inside the flags and take advice from the lifeguards.

Who it suits

  • Learners with a school on smaller days, usually at the south end
  • Improvers wanting consistent waves and lots of coaching choice
  • Experienced surfers on bigger, cleaner days at North Fistral
  • Dog owners, as dogs are allowed all year under control

Think twice if…

  • Beginners heading out alone at the north end
  • Quiet, uncrowded surf on summer weekends or event days
  • Relaxed swimming when the surf is up

Surf guide

Surfing Fistral

Fistral is a sand-bottomed beach break with shifting peaks, and because it faces the open Atlantic it's one of the most consistent spots in the UK. Easterly to south-easterly winds are offshore. North Fistral is mostly rights and is the busiest, most intense part of the beach. The middle has peaks going both ways, and South Fistral, sheltered by the Pentire headland, is usually the best bet for beginners.

At the north end, Little Fistral is a wedgy, reef-like wave over rocks that is not for inexperienced surfers, especially as the tide floods. Beyond it, the Cribbar is a reef off Towan Head that only breaks on big swells and is known as Cornwall's big-wave spot. The banks move, so the best peak changes from week to week. Ask the lifeguards or your instructor where to go.

Level

All levels, in the right place

South Fistral is usually best for beginners; the north end is for confident surfers. Stay in the lesson area on bigger days.

Hazards

Rips and rocks

Rips run along the rocks at the ends of the beach, and the rising tide can catch people out around the south-end rock pools.

Patrol

Long RNLI season

The RNLI listed daily 10am–6pm cover from 3 April to 1 November for 2026. Surf between the black and white flags. Check this year's dates.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Pick the right end

The main Fistral beach car park serves the north end, with sloping sandy access to the beach. For the south end, use the Atlantic Road or Pentire Headland car parks and walk. Check where your school meets before you park.

Access

Dune paths and steps

Paths through the dunes are sandy and unpaved, so keep to the marked routes to protect them. There are steep steps down at the south end.

Facilities

Toilets at both ends

Public toilets at the north and south ends, plus surf shops, hire and food around the beach complex at North Fistral.

Dogs

Allowed all year

Fistral isn't in Cornwall Council's restrictions table, so dogs are allowed year-round. Keep them under control and check signs on the day.

By train

Newquay station

Newquay is the end of the Atlantic Coast Line from Par. Fistral is about a 20-minute walk from the station across town.

FAQs

Fistral: quick answers

Is Fistral good for beginners?

Yes on smaller days with a school, and usually at the south end, which is sheltered by the Pentire headland. The north end is a challenging stretch of water and best left to experienced surfers.

When are lifeguards on Fistral?

The RNLI listed daily 10am–6pm cover from 3 April to 1 November for 2026. Dates can change, so check this year's before you go.

What is the Cribbar?

A reef off Towan Head at the north end of Fistral that only breaks on big swells. It's known as Cornwall's big-wave spot and is strictly for experts.

Where should I park for Fistral?

The main Fistral car park is at the north end. For the south end, the Atlantic Road and Pentire Headland car parks are closer. Match your car park to where your lesson meets.
