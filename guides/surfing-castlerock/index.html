Northern Ireland beach guide

Castlerock at a glance

Castlerock's beach runs for about a kilometre between the sea cliffs of Downhill to the west and the Barmouth, where the River Bann meets the sea, to the east. The village sits right behind it, and Castlerock Golf Club fills the dunes east of the entrance. The beach regularly holds a Blue Flag. On clear days you can look west to the Inishowen peninsula in Donegal.

Four boardwalks lead from the free promenade parking through the dunes, which are protected as a Special Area of Conservation. These dunes run upstream along the estuary to Grangemore, where the National Trust has a bird sanctuary, and include some of the oldest dated dunes in Ireland. Harbour porpoises and seals are often seen feeding in the estuary at the Barmouth, and there is on-site information about the dunes.

It is a quieter base than Portrush, with its own railway station on the Coleraine–Derry~Londonderry line and a regular Blue Flag. Cars can drive onto the eastern part of the beach all year, but wind-blown sand can make it hard going. The concrete pier at the Barmouth is open to walkers and anglers, but the council advises extreme care and says to avoid it in high winds or breaking swell.

Surf guide

Surfing Castlerock

Castlerock is an exposed beach and jetty break with lefts and rights. Forecast sites rate it best on a north-westerly groundswell with offshore winds from the south, and best around mid tide. It is fairly consistent outside summer and is rarely crowded even when it's working.

The Bann estuary is the big thing to understand: keep well away from the river mouth and the pier. Use the flagged zones when lifeguards are on duty in July and August.

Level

Improvers and up

No school on site — learners should book elsewhere.

Conditions

NW swell, south wind, mid tide

Rarely crowded; summer often flat.

Hazards

Bann estuary

River and tidal currents near the Barmouth pier; avoid the pier in big swell.

Facilities

Promenade

Accessible toilets and accessible parking on the promenade.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

The council lists a seasonal RNLI patrol, 10am–6pm daily in July and August. Check the RNLI site for this year's dates.

Dogs & horses

Summer restrictions

The council allows dogs but applies restrictions from 1 June to 15 September — check the signs at the entrance for where they apply. From 1 May to 30 September horse riding is only allowed before 11am and after 7pm.

FAQs

Castlerock: quick answers

Is it safe near the river mouth?

No — keep well clear. The council advises extreme care on the Barmouth pier and not to walk it in high winds or breaking swell.

Is parking free?

Yes, on the promenade.

What conditions suit Castlerock?

Forecast sites rate a north-westerly swell, southerly wind and mid tide.

Can I see wildlife?

Harbour porpoises and seals feed in the estuary at the Barmouth.
