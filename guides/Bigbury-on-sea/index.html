Bigbury-on-Sea at a glance

A gentler South Devon surf option with one of the best backdrops in the county.

Bigbury-on-Sea sits on the western side of the River Avon, directly opposite Bantham and facing the tidal landmark of Burgh Island. The broad sandy beach, shallow areas and facilities close to the sand make it particularly practical for families and first surf trips.

The surf is generally softer and less consistent than Bantham, but that can be exactly the attraction. With enough south-westerly swell, shifting sandbanks produce lefts and rights that can suit foam boards, longboards and progressing surfers.

The important exception is the Avon side of the beach. Currents associated with the river mouth can become strong, so the easiest-looking route towards Bantham is not automatically the safest place to surf.

Surfbooker's take

If Bantham looks too powerful or too competitive, Bigbury is one of the first nearby beaches we'd check. You may lose some wave quality, but gain a much more relaxed learning environment.

Surfbooker local tips

The useful bits before you unload the boards.

More swell towards the Avon

The surf often improves as you move towards the river-mouth side, but the water movement also becomes more important. Better waves do not automatically mean a better place for beginners.

Great alternative to Bantham

If Bantham is too heavy, crowded or intimidating, Bigbury can offer a softer version of the same swell window.

Parking is convenient, not cheap

The main Marine Drive car park is directly above the beach and huge by local standards. The trade-off is a relatively expensive all-day tariff.

Use the card machine

Mobile signal can be unreliable around the beach. The main car park's physical machines take card and contactless, so don't rely entirely on an app.

Dogs still have a summer option

The main beach has seasonal restrictions, but the beach west of the Burgh Island causeway remains dog-friendly throughout the year.

Check the tide for the island

If Burgh Island is part of your day, check the tide before crossing. The causeway disappears quickly and the sea tractor is not a guaranteed public shuttle in all conditions.

Beach & surf guide

Softer than Bantham, but not simply a beginner beach everywhere.

Shifting sandy peaks with the odd better bank

Bigbury is mainly a sandy beach break producing lefts and rights. The banks change, so there is no single guaranteed peak. Smaller days can suit longboards, softboards and learners, while a better bank with enough swell can produce steeper and faster walls.

The beach often works well around mid tide, but tide, sand and swell direction can change the picture considerably. At very small sizes it can lose power, while higher tide can shorten the available ride and push waves closer to shore.

Safety

The river is the hazard that deserves the most attention.

Avon currents

Outgoing and cross-beach currents can strengthen near the River Avon channel.

Rocks

Rocky sections around the edges of the beach become more important as tide and wave direction change.

Main beach patrol

In 2026 the RNLI patrols the main Bigbury beach daily from 11 July to 6 September, 10am to 6pm.

Sedgewell Cove patrol

Sedgewell Cove has a longer 2026 patrol season: daily 16 May to 27 September, 10am to 6pm.

No flags, no lifeguards

Always check which part of the beach is patrolled rather than assuming the whole bay has cover.

Parking

315 spaces directly above the beach.

Marine Drive is one of the easiest surf-beach car parks in the area for carrying boards and family kit, but current charges are at the expensive end.

Pay on entry

The Marine Drive car park operates 24 hours and has 315 spaces, including disabled bays. The site uses card/contactless machines and mobile payment; cash is not accepted at the main machines.

Current published tariff: ?1 up to 30 minutes, ?2 up to 1 hour, ?4.50 up to 2 hours, ?8.50 up to 4 hours, ?12.50 up to 6 hours and ?14.50 all day. Vans and minibuses have a separate ?25 all-day rate. Check the tariff before relying on these figures for a future trip.

Dogs

Seasonal restrictions on the main beach, year-round space west of the causeway.

Dogs are welcome on the main Bigbury beach outside the main summer restriction period. During the summer season, the main bathing beach has restrictions.

The useful bit is that the beach west of the Burgh Island causeway remains dog-friendly throughout the year, so Bigbury can still work for dog owners in peak season.

Check the zone signs

Because Bigbury is split into distinct beach areas around the causeway, follow the signs at the access points rather than assuming one rule covers every stretch of sand.

Burgh Island

The thing that makes Bigbury instantly recognisable.

Walk across at low tide

A sandy tidal causeway links Bigbury-on-Sea to Burgh Island. When exposed, it is an easy short walk, but the sea covers it as the tide rises. Check the tide and leave enough time to return rather than trying to beat the water.

The island itself is open to the public and is home to the historic Pilchard Inn and the Art Deco Burgh Island Hotel, famously associated with Agatha Christie.

Sea tractor

One of the strangest ways to cross a beach in Britain.

When the causeway is underwater, the famous high-legged sea tractor crosses between Bigbury and Burgh Island. The current vehicle dates from 1969 and carries its passengers high above the water while its wheels run across the submerged sand.

The service is primarily for hotel guests. The hotel also allows non-residents using the island's dining facilities to travel for a published ?2 each way, subject to tide, weather and operating conditions. Don't treat it as a guaranteed public ferry without checking first.

Worth seeing even if you don't ride it

The sea tractor is part of the character of Bigbury. If your visit overlaps a rising tide, watching it make the crossing is worth hanging around for.

Walks & scenery

Easy island stroll or a much bigger coastal loop.

The shortest option is Burgh Island itself: the South West Coast Path publishes an easy 0.8-mile circular outing from the Marine Drive car park, tide permitting.

For a proper walk, the Ringmore and Bigbury route covers about 6.5 miles through the Avon Estuary landscape, Ringmore and the clifftops, with access to Challaborough and Ayrmer Cove along the way.

Bigbury works well when not everyone surfs

Someone can surf, someone else can walk the island, and a family can still meet back at the same beach without moving the car.

FAQs

Quick answers before you go.

Is Bigbury-on-Sea good for beginner surfers?

Yes, on suitable days. The sandy bottom and generally softer waves make it one of South Devon's more approachable surf beaches. Beginners should stay away from strong River Avon currents and use the lifeguarded surf area when patrols are operating.

Is there a surf school at Bigbury?

Yes. Discovery Surf School has a permanent base in the lower section of the main beach car park and runs lessons at Bigbury-on-Sea.

Can I hire surfboards and wetsuits?

Yes. Discovery hires surfboards, wetsuits, bodyboards, SUPs and kayaks from its Bigbury base, subject to conditions.

Is Bigbury lifeguarded?

Yes, seasonally. In 2026 the main Bigbury beach is patrolled daily 11 July to 6 September, while Sedgewell Cove has daily cover 16 May to 27 September. Patrol hours are 10am to 6pm.

Are dogs allowed?

The main beach has seasonal summer restrictions, while the beach west of the Burgh Island causeway remains available to dogs throughout the year. Follow current beach-zone signs.

Can anyone walk onto Burgh Island?

Yes. The island is open to the public and can be reached across the sandy causeway when the tide is out. Check the tide before crossing.

Can anyone use the sea tractor?

The sea tractor is primarily used for hotel guests. Non-residents visiting the island's dining facilities can also use it for a fee when it is operating. Weather and tide can prevent operation.

How expensive is parking?

The Marine Drive car park currently charges up to ?14.50 for an all-day car stay, with shorter tariffs available. Check the current tariff before travelling because parking prices can change.
