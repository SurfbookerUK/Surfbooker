Cornwall beach guide

Widemouth Bay at a glance

Widemouth Bay is a wide, open beach about three miles south of Bude, with a small village of mostly twentieth-century bungalows on the gently sloping hill behind it. There is no town here: the bay is backed by low cliffs of Carboniferous sandstone and farmland, and the cafés, pub and shops sit along the beach road and the hill above. Holiday parks and campsites fill in the gaps, which is why it gets busy in summer.

The shape of the beach is what makes it work for surfing. Rocky ledges run out at both ends and between them is a long stretch of sand with banks that shift through the year, so you usually have more than one peak to choose from. At low tide the rock formations around the edges are good for rock-pooling, and the Blue Flag beach has water quality rated excellent.

There is more history here than first appears. Sloops from Wales once used the bay as a basic landing place, unloading coal and limestone and loading Cornish goods for the trip back. On the landward side of the village, a 1960s repeater station marks where several submarine telecom cables come ashore, built to keep running independently with blast doors and its own power. You won't notice it from the sand, but it explains the low, unassuming building behind the bay.

Who it suits

  • Learners having a lesson on the central sandbanks
  • Improvers who want space and a choice of peaks
  • Families who like rock pools as well as the surf
  • Visitors needing level access close to the car park

Think twice if…

  • Summer daytime dog walks on the beach
  • A town day out — Bude is a short drive away
  • Anyone unsure around rocks at high tide

Surf guide

Surfing Widemouth Bay

Widemouth faces west into the Atlantic, so it picks up plenty of swell. Surf-forecast describes it as a mix of beach break, with lefts and rights off the sandbanks, and a right-hand reef break. A west-north-west swell with an easterly, offshore wind gives the best conditions; westerlies blow it out quickly, and it is often crowded when the waves are good.

It can be surfed through the tide, but reviewers on the same page find it often better on the lower stages when the banks come into play. The right-hand side of the bay is rock-strewn, so take care at high tide if you don't know it. Waves are usually manageable for learners on the central banks, although big days do happen.

Best conditions

WNW swell, east wind

Easterly winds are offshore; a west-north-west swell lines it up best.

Tide

Better lower down

Surfable at all states, but the banks tend to work better on the lower tide.

Hazards

Submerged rocks

The rocky right-hand side needs care at high tide; stay between the flags.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Two large car parks

The Beach Guide lists two large car parks with room for over 300 cars, right by the main access.

Facilities

Toilets, cafés, shop

Toilets including accessible facilities, cafés, a beach shop and surf hire cluster around the main entrance.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

RNLI lifeguards patrol in summer. Swim between the red and yellow flags and surf between the black and white.

Dogs

Summer daytime ban

Cornwall Council prohibits dogs from 10am to 6pm between 15 May and 30 September.

Water

Blue Flag beach

The Beach Guide lists a Blue Flag award and excellent water quality.

FAQs

Widemouth Bay: quick answers

Can I take a dog to Widemouth Bay?

Not between 10am and 6pm from 15 May to 30 September, under Cornwall Council's restrictions. Outside those times dogs are allowed.

Is Widemouth good for beginners?

Yes, with a lesson. The central sandbanks usually produce manageable waves, while the reefs at the ends are better left to experienced surfers.

What wind is offshore at Widemouth?

Easterly winds are offshore. The bay faces west, so westerly winds blow it out.

How far is Widemouth from Bude?

About three miles south of the town.
