Northeast England & Yorkshire beach guide

Druridge Bay at a glance

Druridge Bay is a seven-mile curve of sand and dunes between Amble in the north and Cresswell in the south. Druridge Bay Country Park, just off the A1068 about three miles south of Amble near Hadston, gives you the easiest way in, with three car parks, a visitor centre and café beside Ladyburn Lake.

The bay has a striking history. During the Second World War, anti-tank blocks and pillboxes were built along it, and the council warns that wartime debris such as barbed wire can still appear where the dunes erode. Later campaigns saw off a nuclear power station plan in 1989, and parts of the shore are now nature reserves.

For surfing, this is an exposed, open beach with little shelter and no lifeguards. It suits surfers who can read conditions and are happy with space and solitude. The Northumberland Coast Path and National Cycle Network Route 1 both pass through the country park.

Who it suits

  • Surfers who want space
  • Dog walkers
  • Families using the country park and lake
  • Walkers and cyclists on the coast path

Think twice if…

  • Anyone who needs lifeguard cover
  • Shelter from the wind
  • Learners without a school

Surf guide

Surfing Druridge Bay

Druridge Bay is a long, open beach break facing the North Sea. It picks up north-east to east swell, and westerly winds are offshore. Sandbanks form and move along the seven miles, so the best peaks can be anywhere along the bay.

There's no lifeguard on the beach or the lake, and the council advises checking tide times before you go. Long empty stretches mean help can be a long way off, so don't surf alone, and call 999 and ask for the Coastguard in an emergency.

Level

Improvers+

Learners should come with a school.

Hazards

Exposure

Rips, cold water, unstable dunes and long empty stretches. Don't surf alone.

Season

Autumn to spring

North Sea swell is most reliable outside summer; summer is often small but good for lessons.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Country park

Three car parks, the largest next to the visitor centre at the end of the access road. Blue Badge holders park free. Gates open in daylight hours.

Facilities

Visitor centre

Toilets, a café, a small shop, a viewing platform, a playpark and a campsite.

Lifeguards

None

No lifeguard covers the sea or the lake. Check tide times before visiting the beach.

Access

Paths and dunes

Many paths round the lake suit all abilities. The dunes can be unstable, and wartime debris such as barbed wire may be exposed.

Getting there

NE61 5BX

Off the A1068 about three miles south of Amble. The coast path and National Cycle Route 1 run through the park.

FAQs

Druridge Bay: quick answers

Is Druridge Bay lifeguarded?

No. Neither the beach nor Ladyburn Lake has lifeguards.

Where do I park for Druridge Bay?

Druridge Bay Country Park, off the A1068 near Hadston, has three car parks. Blue Badge holders park free.

How long is Druridge Bay?

About seven miles, from Amble to Cresswell.

Can I paddleboard on Ladyburn Lake?

Yes, with a permit from the visitor centre. Sailing, canoeing and windsurfing are allowed too.
