Wales beach guide

Llangennith at a glance

Llangennith Sands is the northern half of the long sweep of sand that runs up from Rhossili to the tidal island of Burry Holms. Together the bay is about 2.8 miles (4.5km) of beach, backed by dunes and by Rhossili Down, whose summit, the Beacon, is the highest point on the Gower. Surfers simply call this end Llangennith, after the village a short way inland.

Because it sits at the westerly tip of the peninsula, it takes the brunt of the Atlantic swell coming up the Bristol Channel. That makes it the Gower's go-to surf beach: when the south-facing bays are flat, there is often still a wave here. The beach shelves gently, so on small days the waves are forgiving enough for learning, and plenty of people take their first lessons here.

You get space rather than services. There is no lifeguard patrol at any time of year, the parking and facilities are at Hillend behind the dunes, and Burry Holms at the northern end can only be reached at low tide. Dogs are welcome all year, which makes it a popular walk outside surf season too. Light easterly winds are offshore, and westerlies blow it out quickly.

Who it suits

  • Surfers of all levels wanting the most consistent waves on the Gower
  • Beginners on small days, ideally with a surf school
  • Campers and van travellers based at Hillend
  • Dog owners — dogs are allowed all year

Think twice if…

  • Anyone who wants lifeguards — Caswell, Langland and Port Eynon are patrolled in summer
  • Late arrivals on sunny weekends hoping for an easy parking space

Surf guide

Surfing Llangennith

Gower's swell magnet.

Llangennith faces west into the open end of the Bristol Channel, so it picks up swell that the south Gower bays miss. With a couple of miles of sand to choose from, the sandbanks shift and you can usually find a peak to yourself by walking a little way from the main path. Light easterly winds are offshore; westerlies blow it out quickly.

The gentle slope gives slow, crumbly waves on smaller days, which is why so many people learn here. As the swell builds, rips form between the banks and the long walk out makes it a serious place to get into trouble with no lifeguards around. On spring low tides look out for the engines of the paddle steamer City of Bristol, wrecked here in the winter of 1840 with the loss of 27 lives.

Level

All levels, sized to the day

Gentle waves suit learners when it is small; bigger days bring stronger rips and are for experienced surfers.

Conditions

West swell, easterly wind

The most exposed beach on the Gower, so often surfable when the south coast is flat.

Hazards

No lifeguards; tidal island

There is no lifeguard service. Burry Holms is cut off as the tide rises — check tide times before you walk out.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Hillend

The main parking is at Hillend behind the dunes, and it fills quickly at weekends and in school holidays.

Facilities

At Hillend, not on the sand

Toilets are by the car park and there is a café at Hillend Caravan Park. The village of Llangennith, with a pub and surf shop, is a short drive inland.

Lifeguards

None

There is no lifeguard service at Llangennith Sands, so take extra care and avoid unfamiliar or rough conditions.

Dogs

Allowed all year

Swansea Council lists Llangennith among the beaches where dogs are allowed all year round.

Island

Burry Holms

The tidal island at the north end is reachable only at low tide. Plan your return before the tide turns.

FAQs

Llangennith: quick answers

Are there lifeguards at Llangennith?

No. There is no lifeguard service at any time of year, so beginners are best going with a surf school.

Can I take my dog to Llangennith beach in summer?

Yes. Swansea Council allows dogs on Llangennith all year round.

Is Llangennith the same beach as Rhossili?

Yes. It is the northern half of the same long beach; Rhossili village and Worm's Head are at the southern end.

Where do I park?

At Hillend, behind the dunes. Get there early on sunny weekends.
