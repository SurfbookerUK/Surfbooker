Northeast England & Yorkshire beach guide

Sandsend at a glance

Sandsend is the small village at the north-western end of the long sands from Whitby, sitting at the foot of Lythe Bank. Two streams, Sandsend Beck and East Row Beck, run down through Mulgrave Woods and out across the sand. The two hamlets of Sandsend and East Row grew together when cottages were built for workers in the local alum industry.

The coastal railway between Whitby and Middlesbrough once ran through here on high viaducts over the becks, and the Cleveland Way now follows the old line north out of the village. The RNLI describes it as a small, picturesque beach on Yorkshire's heritage coast, popular with fossil hunters and anglers, with the cliffs giving some shelter.

For surfers, the appeal is a friendly beach break that the National Park lists among the good spots for beginners, with cafés, a shop and a pub close to the sand and lifeguards in high summer. It's also handy as a quieter alternative to Whitby when West Cliff is busy, and you can walk back along the sands to Whitby when the tide allows.

Who it suits

  • Beginners
  • Families wanting a smaller village beach
  • A quieter alternative to Whitby's West Cliff
  • Fossil hunters and walkers

Think twice if…

  • Experienced surfers looking for power
  • Dog walkers wanting the southern beach near the village in summer

Surf guide

Surfing Sandsend

Sandsend is a sandy beach break. Surf-forecast calls it reasonably exposed with quite reliable surf, best on a north-north-east swell with westerly offshore winds, and surfable at all stages of the tide. That consistency, plus the gentle sand, is why the National Park recommends it for beginners.

Surf-forecast also says it's often crowded when there's a wave, so give learners space. Lifeguard cover here is more limited than in Whitby outside the school holidays, so check the RNLI dates before you paddle out.

Level

Beginners

The National Park lists Sandsend among the good beginner beach breaks.

Conditions

NNE swell, westerly wind

Surf-forecast rates north-north-east swell with west winds best; works at all tides.

Hazards

Becks and tide

Two becks cross the beach, and the walk along the sands to Whitby depends on the tide.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Lifeguards

RNLI, 10am–6pm

In 2026 the RNLI covered weekends from 2 to 17 May, daily over May half term (23–31 May), weekends only from 6 June to 5 July, and daily from 11 July to 6 September.

Dogs

Partly banned 1 May–30 Sep

Dogs are banned from the beach between the steps below the public toilets at Sandhills and the slipway at the south end of Bank Bottom car park, every year from 1 May to 30 September.

Facilities

In the village

The council lists a café, toilets, a shop and a nearby pub.

Walks

Cleveland Way

The Cleveland Way runs through the village and follows the old railway line north towards Runswick Bay, five miles away.

Getting here

A174

Sandsend is on the A174 coast road just north of Whitby; parking is in the village and at Lythe Bank.

FAQs

Sandsend: quick answers

Is Sandsend good for beginners?

Yes. The North York Moors National Park lists Sandsend as having good beach breaks for beginners, and surf-forecast describes the surf as quite reliable at all stages of the tide.

When is Sandsend lifeguarded?

In 2026, daily from 11 July to 6 September, plus weekends in May and June and daily over May half term, 10am to 6pm.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, except on the section between the Sandhills toilet steps and the Bank Bottom car park slipway from 1 May to 30 September.

Can I walk from Whitby to Sandsend?

Yes, along the sands from West Cliff Beach — check tide times first.
