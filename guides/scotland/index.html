Scotland surf & beach guides

Choose a destination

Compare Scotland’s surf regions honestly.

Experienced surfers

Thurso East

Research-based take: Thurso East is genuinely one of Europe's standout cold-water reef waves and a destination in its own right. It is also a poor choice for beginners: shallow reef, a concentrated take-off and serious crowd dynamics make it an advanced/expert surf rather than a sightseeing-led 'have a go' beach.

Improvers and experienced surfers

Thurso Beach

Research-based take: Thurso Beach is a useful, accessible town beach rather than the reason most surfers travel to Thurso. On a clean north to north-west swell it can produce fun lefts and rights over sand, and it is much less consequential than Thurso East, but visitors should not assume that means it is always beginner-safe.

Mixed abilities on suitable days

Dunnet Bay

Research-based take: one of the best beginner-to-intermediate surf bases on Scotland's north coast. The bay is scenic, accessible by north-coast standards and backed by a genuinely strong local operator, while still being close enough to Thurso for food and accommodation.

Improvers and experienced surfers

Melvich

Research-based take: a rewarding north-coast surf stop for intermediates who value quiet beaches and natural scenery more than facilities. It can produce long, good-quality waves around the river-fed sandbar, but remoteness and rips make it less suitable as a first-ever surf destination.

Beginners to experienced

Pease Bay

Research-based take: one of south-east Scotland's better quality, more surf-focused beach breaks, especially on a clean north-east swell. It is more interesting for intermediates than Belhaven, but the defined peaks, rocks and crowd pressure mean it is not automatically the easiest beginner destination.

Beginners and longboarders

Belhaven Bay

Research-based take: one of Scotland's easiest surf destinations to recommend to ordinary visitors because it combines a forgiving sandy bay, an established surf school, proper parking/toilets/showers and the amenities of Dunbar. It is more of a reliable learning/longboard destination than a high-performance surf trip.

Beginners to improvers

Coldingham Bay

Research-based take: probably the most complete small-bay surf destination in south-east Scotland. It is scenic, family-friendly, has a long-established beachfront surf school/hire centre and, unlike most Scottish surf beaches, has seasonal RNLI lifeguards.

Improvers and experienced surfers

Fraserburgh

Research-based take: a genuinely strong east-coast surf destination that is more varied than many guides suggest. The long beach offers accessible peaks, while the harbour end can produce a quality point-style wave for better surfers. The town also gives it better practical infrastructure than most remote Scottish breaks.

Mixed abilities on suitable days

Lossiemouth

Research-based take: a very user-friendly Scottish surf town. East Beach has a broad sandy setup, a permanent local surf school and a real town immediately behind it, so it works exceptionally well for beginners, families and weekend visitors who do not want a remote expedition.

Improvers and experienced surfers

Machrihanish

Research-based take: one of Scotland's most scenic west-coast surf areas, but more fickle than its Atlantic-facing location makes it appear. When a proper west groundswell arrives it can be excellent; when it misses the narrow window it can be frustrating. The big strength is having Machrihanish and Westport close together.

Improvers and experienced surfers

Balevullin, Tiree

Research-based take: one of the strongest all-round surf destinations on this list. It combines genuine Atlantic exposure, beautiful surroundings, a relaxed island atmosphere and an unusually useful on-beach surf school. It can suit first-timers on the right day but still offers quality for experienced surfers.

Experienced, self-sufficient surfers

Dalmore Bay, Lewis

Research-based take: a beautiful, exposed Atlantic surf beach with far more power and remoteness than its sandy appearance suggests. It is a strong destination for competent surfers and a superb scenic stop, but I would not market it as a casual family surf beach without qualifications.
