Northern Ireland beach guide

Portrush East Strand at a glance

East Strand is Portrush's long town beach, around 2.5km of sand running east from the promenade until it merges with Curran Strand and finally Whiterocks. Behind it sits a big dune system that holds the Royal Portrush golf links, and out to sea you look across to the Skerries and the Causeway headlands. On the grass bank by the beach is Holger Lonze's sculpture 'To the People of the Sea', a tribute to local boatbuilder James Kelly.

For surfing it is the most practical beach on the north coast. The council describes it as busy with watersports all year round, surfing above all, and the RNLI says surfers of every level use it. You get a large charged car park, toilets, cold outdoor showers and ramps onto the sand at both ends of the promenade, with the town's cafés and the railway station a short walk away.

Because the beach is so long you can usually walk away from the crowd near the town end. It is also popular with racehorse trainers, who exercise horses between Whiterocks and East Strand, so keep an eye out early and late in the day. Look out for gannets, eider, guillemots and terns offshore, and expect the beach to fill up quickly on warm summer weekends.

Who it suits

  • First lessons and families who want everything close by
  • Car-free trips — the station is in town
  • Improvers who want room to find their own peak
  • Walkers heading east along the sand to Whiterocks

Think twice if…

  • Riding or walking dogs freely on summer days — seasonal restrictions apply
  • A quiet beach on hot summer weekends

Surf guide

Surfing Portrush East Strand

East Strand is an exposed sandy beach break with peaks going both left and right. Forecast sites rate the Portrush area best on a north-westerly groundswell with offshore winds from the south. Summer is often flat or small, while autumn through to spring brings more consistent swell. Banks shift with storms, so the best spot along the beach changes from week to week.

The RNLI has reported lifeguards rescuing a bodyboarder caught in a flash rip here, so check with the lifeguards before you paddle out and stay inside the flagged zones when they are on duty. Further east the beach gets more exposed, and Whiterocks at the far end usually picks up a little more swell.

Level

All abilities

Gentle days suit lessons; bigger winter swells are for experienced surfers.

Conditions

NW swell, southerly wind

Forecast sites favour north-west groundswell and a south wind; summer is often flat.

Hazards

Flash rips

The RNLI has reported rescues from flash rips. Watch for horses and other water users too.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Large charged car park

About 637 spaces, charged daily. Concrete ramps lead onto the sand at both ends of the promenade.

Facilities

Toilets and showers

Toilets (including at the Watersports Centre), accessible toilets and parking, and outdoor cold showers. Beach access equipment can be pre-booked through the Mae Murray Foundation.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

In 2026 the RNLI patrolled 10am–6pm over the Easter holidays, at weekends from mid-April to late June, daily from 27 June to 31 August and at weekends through September. No flags means no lifeguards.

Dogs & horses

Summer restrictions

The council allows dogs but applies restrictions from 1 June to 15 September — check the signs at the entrance for where they apply. From 1 May to 30 September horse riding is only allowed before 11am and after 7pm.

FAQs

Portrush East Strand: quick answers

Is Portrush East Strand good for beginners?

Yes, on small days and inside the flagged zone when lifeguards are on duty. If you are new, take a lesson rather than going out alone.

When are lifeguards on duty?

In 2026 the RNLI patrolled daily from 27 June to 31 August, 10am–6pm, plus Easter, spring weekends and September weekends. Check the RNLI page for this year.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, but the council applies restrictions from 1 June to 15 September.

Can I walk to Whiterocks?

Yes — the sand runs continuously east via Curran Strand to Whiterocks.
