Northeast England & Yorkshire beach guide

Filey Bay at a glance

Filey Bay is a wide, gently curving bay with around five miles of golden sand, stretching south from the town towards Flamborough. At the town end the beach is backed by a historic promenade with beach chalets and a sculpture trail, and the Coble Landing is where Filey's traditional fishing boats, the cobles, are still kept. The Royal Crescent above the beach dates from the 1850s.

To the north the bay ends at Filey Brigg, a long, narrow rocky peninsula about a mile from town. The landward end, Carr Naze, was the site of a Roman signal station, and the rocks and shore are a Site of Special Scientific Interest that draws wading birds such as purple sandpipers in winter. The Brigg also marks the end of the Cleveland Way.

Filey is a classic family beach first and a surf spot second. Surf-forecast calls it an exposed beach break with unreliable waves, so it pays to check a forecast before driving over. When it does work, it's usually quiet in the water. The best of it tends to come on a north-north-east swell around mid tide, and the rips here deserve respect.

Who it suits

  • Families wanting space and a promenade
  • Learners and improvers on the occasional good day
  • Surfers looking for an uncrowded alternative to Scarborough
  • Walkers combining a session with the Brigg or the Cleveland Way

Think twice if…

  • Anyone who needs reliable surf
  • Dog walkers wanting the town-end beach in summer

Surf guide

Surfing Filey Bay

Filey is a long sandy beach break. Surf-forecast describes the waves as unreliable, best on a north-north-east swell around mid tide, with north-westerly to westerly winds. Because the Brigg sits to the north, the bay can pick up less swell than more exposed beaches, so small days are common.

The main hazard surf-forecast highlights is rips, which it describes as dangerous. Stay between the red and yellow flags in summer and avoid paddling out near the rocks of the Brigg. On the plus side, the line-up is rarely busy even when there are waves.

Level

Beginners to improvers

Gentle sand when there's a wave, but conditions are inconsistent.

Conditions

NNE swell, mid tide

Surf-forecast rates north-north-east swell, mid tide and north-west to west winds as best.

Hazards

Rips and the Brigg

Surf-forecast warns of dangerous rips; the Brigg's clay cliffs at Carr Naze are actively eroding.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Lifeguards

RNLI, 10am–6pm

In 2026 RNLI lifeguards covered weekends and bank holidays from 2 to 17 May, then patrolled daily from 23 May to 6 September.

Dogs

Town end banned 1 May–30 Sep

The council's order bans dogs from the beach up to the slipway that separates the Coble Landing from the promenade every year from 1 May to 30 September. Dogs must be on a lead year-round on the promenade from the south end of Coble Landing to the sea wall at Royal Parade.

Facilities

At the town end

The council lists cafés, toilets, disabled facilities, a first aid point, a lost child centre, a shop, and deckchair and beach hut hire.

The Brigg

Rock pools and walks

Filey Brigg has good rock pools to explore, and it's where the Cleveland Way ends. Filey is also the northern end of the Yorkshire Wolds Way.

Getting here

Filey station

Filey station is on the Yorkshire Coast Line between Scarborough and Hull.

FAQs

Filey Bay: quick answers

Is Filey good for surfing?

Sometimes. Surf-forecast describes Filey as an exposed beach break with unreliable waves, best on a north-north-east swell around mid tide. When it works it's usually uncrowded.

Are dogs allowed on Filey beach?

Not on the town-end section between 1 May and 30 September. Outside that zone and those dates dogs are allowed, but they must be on a lead on the promenade all year.

Is Filey beach lifeguarded?

Yes, in season. In 2026 RNLI lifeguards patrolled at weekends in early May and daily from 23 May to 6 September, 10am to 6pm.

What is Filey Brigg?

A long, narrow rocky peninsula at the north end of the bay. It has rock pools, a Roman signal station site at Carr Naze, and is protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest.
