Worth leaving the M5 for

Clevedon

A proper break in the journey: Victorian seafront, historic pier, open-air swimming, an independent cinema and far better lunch options than another service station.

M5 junction 20

Turn a travel break into part of the trip.

We stumbled across Clevedon by chance after the doctor advised us to take plenty of leg-stretching breaks on our journeys to the coast. It quickly became one of our favourite places to pause because the stop feels like part of the trip rather than time lost beside the motorway.

Clevedon is far easier to access and much less busy and bustling than Bristol. It is only a few minutes from M5 junction 20, so it is hardly a diversion, and you can continue to the seafront for a walk, coffee or swim. It is practical too: Aldi is right beside the junction and Lidl on Great Western Road is also only around two or three minutes away in our experience, making it easy to pick up supplies before continuing south.

Our favourite food stop

About three minutes from the junction in our experience. It has an enormous all-day menu, welcomes dogs and costs roughly what we would expect to spend at a motorway service station, but gives us a much better break.

Our verdict

Worth the stop

Allow around an hour for lunch, or two to three hours if you want to include the pier and seafront.

The essentials

What to see and do.

The attractions are varied enough for a quick leg-stretch, a long lunch or half a day away from the motorway.

Clevedon Pier

Opened in 1869, the elegant iron pier is the only operating Grade I-listed seaside pier in the UK. One Direction also filmed the music video for “You & I” here in 2014. Walk to the pagoda at the end for wide Bristol Channel views. Admission and weather restrictions can apply, so check before visiting.

Marine Lake

The tidal lake opened in 1929 to offer safer salt-water swimming away from the Channel's strong currents. Today it is used for swimming, paddleboarding, kayaking, crabbing and model boating.

Curzon Cinema

One of the country's oldest continuously operating purpose-built cinemas. It opened in 1912 and remains community owned, with films, events and more than a century of cinema history.

The seafront

Walk between the pier, pebbled beach, Salthouse Fields and the marine lake. The exposed Channel setting can be wild and dramatic when the weather turns.

Hill Road

A handsome Victorian shopping street with independent cafés, shops and galleries. It grew with the town's nineteenth-century transformation into a seaside resort.

Clevedon Court

A medieval manor house dating from around 1320 and associated with the Elton family since 1709. National Trust opening is limited, so plan this one rather than arriving speculatively.

Food and drink

Better than the services.

Clevedon works especially well because you can choose between an easy all-day restaurant close to junction 20 and a longer seafront stop with independent cafés and pubs.

Teatro Lounge

Our favourite restaurant for a quick M5 stop. The tuna melt toastie is a lunch favourite, the menu covers almost every appetite and dogs are welcome.

Moon & Sixpence

Our pub pick by the marine lake. The Moon & Sixpence has much friendlier staff and far better service than the Salthouse. It is also dog friendly, has its own car park and serves a particularly good Sunday roast.

Coffee and cafés

Tiffin at the Beach has uninterrupted Channel views, while Hill Road and the town centre add independents including Mustard, Fawn Coffee and Books on the Hill Tea Room. Check current hours before travelling.

From manor to resort

A short history of Clevedon.

Clevedon's appeal is easier to understand when you see how an agricultural settlement became a fashionable Victorian seaside town, then fought to preserve its defining landmarks.

1086-1320

Medieval roots

Clevedon appears in the Domesday Survey. Sir John de Clevedon built Clevedon Court around 1320, incorporating an earlier medieval tower.

1709 onwards

The Elton family

Abraham Elton bought Clevedon Court in 1709. Later generations reshaped the estate and invested in the town, though the family's wealth also had direct links to Bristol commerce and the transatlantic slave trade.

1820s-1850s

A seaside town emerges

The Elton estate sold building plots and lodging houses appeared on the slopes with views towards the Channel. The railway branch from Yatton opened in 1847, making visits and commuting easier.

1869

The pier opens

Clevedon Pier opened on Easter Monday as a landing point for steamers to South Wales. Its slender spans reuse wrought-iron railway material and were designed around the Channel's exceptional tidal range.

1912-1929

Cinema and open-air swimming

The Curzon cinema opened in 1912. Clevedon Marine Lake followed in 1929, offering a tidal pool refreshed by high water and safer bathing than the open Channel.

1970-1999

The pier is saved

Two pier spans collapsed during load testing in 1970. A determined preservation campaign defeated demolition, and decades of restoration culminated in the complete pier reopening in 1998.

Our top tip

Come in summer. Come back in winter.

Clevedon is great in summer when the marine lake, promenade and outdoor tables come into their own. It is just as compelling in winter: the pier, monochrome water and weather rolling across the Bristol Channel give the seafront a completely different, atmospheric character.
