Bantham Beach

Waves, tides, beginner suitability, parking, facilities and the dangers to know before you visit.

Updated 2 August 2026

One of South Devon's best-known surf beaches.

Bantham is one of South Devon's best-known surf beaches. It sits beside the mouth of the River Avon, with views across Bigbury Bay towards Burgh Island.

The beach can produce some of the best-shaped waves on Devon's south coast, although it is more tide-dependent and generally less consistent than the major Devon north-coast beaches.

What is the surfing like?

Bantham is a sandy beach and river-mouth break capable of producing both left- and right-breaking waves.

The shifting sandbanks around the River Avon can create surprisingly powerful, well-shaped waves, particularly when a south-westerly or westerly swell combines with an easterly or north-easterly wind.

Conditions are generally best between low and mid tide. At high tide, the waves can lose their shape or break heavily close to the beach.

The river-mouth section can offer the best waves, but it also has the strongest currents and should be left to experienced surfers when conditions are powerful.

Is Bantham good for beginners?

Bantham can be good for beginners when the waves are small, particularly for practising in the whitewater. However, it is not somewhere complete beginners should surf without guidance when the river is flowing strongly.

A permanent river channel runs along the southern side of the beach. The current can pull surfers away from the main beach surprisingly quickly, particularly during an outgoing tide.

Beginners should stay well away from the river mouth, use the appropriate surfing area between the black-and-white flags and consider booking a lesson for their first visit.

RNLI lifeguards patrol Bantham during the main season, including daily cover from May until late September in 2026. No flags displayed means that lifeguards are not operating. Check current Bantham lifeguard dates

Parking

Bantham has a large private car park with space for more than 1,000 vehicles. The beach is approximately 75 metres from the car park, so carrying boards and wetsuits is relatively easy.

Current 2026 parking charges are:

  • £12 for the full day
  • £9 when arriving after 1pm
  • £6 when arriving after 4pm
  • £50 for a weekly ticket
  • £85 for two weeks

The car park is convenient but expensive if you only want a short surf. Roads approaching Bantham are also narrow, and traffic can become slow during sunny weekends and school holidays. Check current Bantham parking information

Facilities and atmosphere

Bantham has toilets, surf equipment hire and seasonal food and drink close to the beach. Food trucks in the car park serve coffee, breakfast, lunches, cakes and ice cream, while the Sloop Inn and Bantham Village Stores are a short walk away. Bantham food and drink information

The setting is one of Bantham's biggest strengths. It feels scenic and relatively undeveloped, with dunes, the River Avon and views towards Burgh Island.

It is quieter in character than Woolacombe or Croyde, although the water can still become crowded when the forecast is good.

The drawbacks

The river current is Bantham's most important hazard. It can be useful for experienced surfers paddling out, but beginners should not treat it as an easy route through the breaking waves.

The surf is also less reliable than on Devon's north coast. Bantham can be excellent when the swell direction, wind and tide line up, but it can be small or completely flat, particularly during summer.

Parking is expensive, the approach roads can become congested and the most popular part of the break can feel crowded.

Conditions change noticeably with the tide, so checking the forecast and tide times before travelling is especially important.

Surfbooker verdict

Bantham is one of the strongest surfing options in South Devon. On the right day, its sandbanks can produce clean, enjoyable waves for improving and experienced surfers, while the whitewater can suit beginners receiving proper instruction.

Its beautiful setting, nearby facilities and short walk from the car park make it an excellent place for a full beach day.

However, absolute beginners must be cautious around the River Avon, and anyone travelling a long distance should remember that the south-coast swell is less dependable than Devon's north coast.
