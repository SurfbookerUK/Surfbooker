Cornwall beach guide

Sennen Cove at a glance

Sennen is the beach at the very end of the A30, about a mile north of Land's End, and the most westerly surf beach on the British mainland. It sits at the southern end of Whitesand Bay (not to be confused with Whitsand Bay near Plymouth), with a small harbour, the lifeboat station, cafés and galleries tucked under the Pedn-men-du headland. That headland shelters the cove itself from westerly gales, while the rest of the bay lies wide open to the Atlantic.

There's sand here at every state of the tide, but the beach changes a lot through the day. As the tide drops, a big expanse of pale sand appears and Sennen joins up with Gwynver to the north, giving roughly a mile of continuous beach. The sand is mostly crushed shell, particularly clam, rather than ground-down granite. Behind the bay, nearly 100 acres of dunes run between Pedn-men-du and Aire Point and are an important habitat for rare insects.

Sennen picks up swell when much of Cornwall is flat, so the postcard setting doesn't make every day a beginner's day. The waves are usually smallest at the southern end near the harbour and build as you move north. RNLI lifeguards patrol in season and move the flags as the tide changes to show where it's safest to swim, so check where they are each time you arrive rather than heading back to yesterday's spot.

Who it suits

  • Surfers chasing swell when the rest of Cornwall is flat
  • Lessons at the smaller southern end on small days
  • Coast-path walkers heading to Land's End or Gwynver
  • Families who want toilets and cafés close to the sand

Think twice if…

  • Dog walkers on summer days (15 May to 30 September, 10am to 6pm)
  • Novices on big or windy days, or anywhere near the offshore reef

Surf guide

Surfing Sennen Cove

Sennen is fully exposed to the Atlantic, and its position close to Land's End means it can pick up swell from a wider range of directions than most north-coast beaches. Wave size grades along the beach: smallest at the harbour end in the south, biggest towards the north end, where the more experienced crowd tends to sit. At high tide expect a shorebreak; as the tide drops the beach opens up and there's far more room.

Light north to north-easterly winds are the ones to look for. Around 100 metres offshore is a reef known as the Cowloes, which experienced surfers ride on the right day, but the rocks make it no place for novices. Peaks shift with the sandbanks and there are rocks at both ends of the beach, so spend a few minutes watching before you paddle out.

Level

Beginners to experienced

Small days at the southern end suit lessons; the north end and the Cowloes reef are for experienced surfers.

Wind

North to north-easterly

The favoured wind direction for clean waves. Check the forecast before you set off down the A30.

Hazards

Rocks, reef and shorebreak

Rocks at each end of the beach and on the offshore reef, plus a dumping shorebreak around high tide.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Cove and hill-top car parks

Two car parks sit at the bottom of the hill, one behind the beach and one beyond the lifeboat station, with a large field car park at the top of the hill in Sennen village. They're shared with the village and fill up in peak season.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

Swim between the red and yellow flags and surf between the black and white flags. The flags move with the tide, and patrol dates change each year, so check the RNLI website.

Dogs

Summer daytime ban

Cornwall Council prohibits dogs from Sennen beach from 10am to 6pm, 15 May to 30 September. Outside those times dogs are welcome.

Facilities

Harbour village

Public toilets, including an accessible toilet, a shop and several cafés are close to the beach. The lifeboat station has a viewing gallery.

Getting there

A30 or the Land's End Coaster

By car, follow the A30 almost to its end. By public transport, the Land's End Coaster bus (routes 1 and 1A) runs from Penzance via Land's End to Sennen; check current timetables.

FAQs

Sennen Cove: quick answers

Can dogs go on Sennen beach?

Not between 10am and 6pm from 15 May to 30 September. Outside those times, and the rest of the year, dogs are allowed.

Is Sennen good for beginners?

On small, clean days, yes. The southern end near the harbour usually has the smallest waves and is where lessons tend to happen. On big or windy days it's a beach for experienced surfers, so stay within the lifeguarded areas.

Is there anywhere quieter nearby?

Gwynver, at the northern end of Whitesand Bay, is popular with surfers and usually quieter. You can walk there along the South West Coast Path, and at low tide the two beaches join up.

Is Whitesand Bay the same as Whitsand Bay?

No. Whitesand Bay is the bay at Sennen near Land's End. Whitsand Bay is a different stretch of coast near Plymouth in south-east Cornwall.
