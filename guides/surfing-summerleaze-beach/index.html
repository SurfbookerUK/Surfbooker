Cornwall beach guide

Summerleaze at a glance

Summerleaze is the beach right in front of Bude, at the point where the River Neet and the Bude Canal meet the sea. At low tide it is a huge expanse of sand stretching out past the breakwater; at high tide it shrinks back towards the sea pool and the beach huts.

It is the easiest beach in north Cornwall to combine a surf lesson with a full family day: the town's cafés, shops and toilets are within a few minutes' walk, and Bude Sea Pool — a partly natural tidal pool built into the rocks — gives sheltered swimming when the sea is rough.

The river mouth, the breakwater and moving sandbanks mean the surf changes a lot through the tide, so use the lifeguarded zones and let a school pick the spot if you are new.

Who it suits

  • Families mixing surf, sea-pool swimming and town
  • First and second surf lessons
  • Car-free visits within Bude

Think twice if…

  • Dog walkers wanting off-lead space in summer daytime
  • Crowd-free surf on summer weekends

Surf guide

Surfing Summerleaze

Summerleaze faces west and picks up Atlantic swell, but the breakwater and Compass Point give the northern side some shelter. Banks form around the river channel and along the beach, so the best spot changes from week to week.

Light easterly winds are offshore. On bigger days the river mouth can create strong currents — keep within the flagged areas.

Level

Beginners to improvers

Two established schools teach here on suitable days.

Hazards

River mouth and breakwater

Keep clear of the channel and harbour activity, especially on an outgoing tide.

Swimming

Bude Sea Pool

A separate tidal pool in the rocks — check its own opening and safety information.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Summerleaze car park & alternatives

Summerleaze car park is right beside the beach; the Wharf car park and Bude's seasonal park-and-ride (which stops at Summerleaze and Crooklets) help on busy days.

Facilities

Town on the doorstep

Public toilets with accessible facilities, cafés and shops are close to the beach.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

Swim between the red and yellow flags and surf between the black and white flags.

Dogs

On leads in summer daytime

Cornwall Council requires dogs on leads from 21 May to 30 September, 10am–6pm.

FAQs

Summerleaze: quick answers

Can dogs go on Summerleaze beach?

Yes, but they must be on leads between 10am and 6pm from 21 May to 30 September.

Is Summerleaze good for learning to surf?

Yes on suitable days — Big Blue Surf School and Bude Surfing Experience both teach here, and there's a seasonal lifeguard patrol.

Where should I park?

Summerleaze car park is beside the beach. On busy summer days the park-and-ride is the easier option.
