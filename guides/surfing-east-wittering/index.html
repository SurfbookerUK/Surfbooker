Southern England beach guide

East Wittering at a glance

East Wittering is a large village on the Manhood Peninsula, about seven miles south-west of Chichester, with Bracklesham running on to the east and West Wittering to the west. The beach is shingle at the top with sand appearing as the tide drops, broken up by wooden groynes. The council car park on Marine Drive is next to the beach, and the village shops, cafés and pubs are a short walk back from the front.

There's been a settlement here for more than a thousand years. The Witterings were part of a land grant to Bishop Wilfrid in the late 7th century, and Wittering appears in the Domesday Book. In May 1944 the beach hosted a D-Day rehearsal, when the 3rd Canadian Infantry Division landed here during Operation Fabius. East Wittering is also one of England's 'Thankful Villages', which lost no residents in the First World War.

For surfers it's a local beach rather than a destination. When a south-westerly swell pushes up the Channel, usually in autumn and winter, the groynes and sandbanks give peaks along the front. There are no lifeguards, so for patrolled swimming in summer head to West Wittering. The council describes the beach as gently sloping and free of rip currents, but warns that waves can get big at high tide.

Surf guide

Surfing East Wittering

Spot guides class East Wittering as an inconsistent beach and groyne break, with lefts and rights on a south-westerly swell. Short-period windswell is far more common than clean groundswell, so it pays to watch for a swell that holds as the wind drops or swings north-easterly, which is offshore. Forecast sites suggest it often works best on a rising tide towards high.

When it's on, it gets busy, as plenty of local surfers are waiting for the same few days. Stay well clear of the groynes. Surf forecasters flag rips here too, while the council says the beach generally has none but that big waves at high tide can make it hard to get out over the steep shingle. With no lifeguards, it's best for surfers who can judge conditions for themselves.

Season

Autumn and winter

Channel swell is most likely from autumn to early spring. Summer is usually small and much busier.

Hazards

Groynes and shingle

Keep clear of the groynes and take care getting in and out over steep shingle when waves are big at high tide.

Conditions

SW swell, NE wind

A south-westerly swell with a north-easterly wind is the classic combination. Forecasters favour a rising tide.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Marine Drive car park

Chichester District Council's pay car park on Marine Drive is beside the beach, with disabled spaces. More options are in the village.

Access

Shingle with a footpath alongside

The shingle makes access difficult for wheelchair users, though there's a footpath next to the beach.

Lifeguards

None

There are no lifeguards; the council recommends West Wittering for lifeguarded swimming in high season. Foreshore officers work from Easter to the end of September. In an emergency, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.

Dogs

Allowed all year

The council allows well-behaved dogs anywhere on East Wittering beach; pick up after them. The district's dog control order is being renewed in autumn 2026, so check for changes.

By bus

Stagecoach 52/53

Stagecoach's 52/53 runs a loop from Chichester through Birdham, Bracklesham, East Wittering and West Wittering. Check current timetables.

FAQs

East Wittering: quick answers

When is the best time to surf East Wittering?

Autumn and winter, when a south-westerly swell is most likely. Ideally you want it to coincide with a light or north-easterly wind.

Is East Wittering beach lifeguarded?

No. For a lifeguarded beach in high season, the council points visitors to West Wittering.

Can dogs go on East Wittering beach?

Yes. Chichester District Council allows well-behaved dogs on the whole beach all year, as long as you clear up after them. Check for any changes under the council's new dog control order.

How do I get to East Wittering without a car?

Take the train to Chichester, then Stagecoach's 52/53 bus, which loops through the Witterings and Bracklesham.
