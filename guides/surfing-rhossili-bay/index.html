Wales beach guide

Rhossili Bay at a glance

Rhossili Bay curves for about 2.8 miles (4.5km) along the western tip of the Gower, from the clifftop village of Rhossili in the south to the tidal island of Burry Holms in the north. Dunes back the sand, and above them rises Rhossili Down, whose summit, the Beacon, at 193 metres, is the highest point on the peninsula, with wide views over the whole bay.

At the southern end, Worm's Head reaches out to sea as two tidal islands, Inner Head and Outer Head, connected to the mainland only around low tide. It is one of the most photographed views in Wales, and in 2014 TripAdvisor users voted the bay the best beach in the UK. The crossing times are posted at the Coastguard lookout above the causeway, and it is the island most visitors come to see.

Getting down from the village is a steep path with flights of steps, which keeps this end quieter than the view suggests. At low tide the ribs of the Helvetia, wrecked in 1887, show through the sand. There are no lifeguards, but dogs are welcome all year. The village car park, shop, pub and National Trust visitor centre are all at the top of the path, so bring what you need down with you.

Who it suits

  • Walkers and photographers
  • Families happy with a steep walk down and back up
  • Experienced surfers looking for space
  • Dog owners — dogs are allowed all year

Think twice if…

  • Pushchairs or limited mobility — the path and steps are steep
  • Unsupervised beginner surfers — there is no lifeguard service

Surf guide

Surfing Rhossili Bay

Rhossili is one of the most exposed beaches in Wales and picks up any Atlantic swell running up the Bristol Channel. The southern end, below the village, gets a little shelter from Worm's Head, but most surfers drive round to Hillend and surf the Llangennith end, which is a much shorter walk from the car.

Light easterly winds are offshore along the whole bay. With no lifeguards and a long climb back up to the village, surf this end only if you are confident in the conditions.

Level

Experienced, or with a school

No lifeguard service and a long walk back up if anything goes wrong.

Conditions

West swell, easterly wind

Exposed to the open Atlantic; the Llangennith end is the usual surf spot.

Hazards

Worm's Head tides

The causeway is open only around low tide. Check the crossing times at the Coastguard lookout and never try to swim back.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Village car park

The car park at the end of Rhossili village is charged and fills on sunny weekends.

Access

Steep path and steps

It is a steep path with several flights of steps from the village to the sand.

Facilities

In the village

A shop, pub, hotel and National Trust visitor centre are at the top of the path; there is nothing on the beach itself.

Lifeguards

None

There is no lifeguard service at Rhossili Bay.

Dogs

Allowed all year

Swansea Council lists Rhossili among the beaches where dogs are allowed all year round. Keep them under control near livestock on the Down.

FAQs

Rhossili Bay: quick answers

Can you surf at Rhossili?

Yes, it is very exposed and picks up plenty of swell, but there are no lifeguards. Most surfers use the Llangennith end, closer to parking at Hillend.

When can I walk to Worm's Head?

Only around low tide. Crossing times are posted at the Coastguard lookout above the causeway.

Are dogs allowed on Rhossili beach?

Yes, all year round, according to Swansea Council.

How long is Rhossili beach?

About 2.8 miles (4.5km), running north to Burry Holms and the Llangennith end.
