Northeast England & Yorkshire beach guide

Redcar at a glance

Redcar's beach runs along the town seafront from Coatham towards Marske, and the RNLI describes it as a busy, gently sloping sandy resort beach close to shops, restaurants and pubs. It's popular for kitesurfing and windsurfing as well as surfing, so the water is often shared.

Rock scars uncover at low tide and shape several of the peaks, so the surf changes a lot through the tide and with swell direction. It's a convenient local option when conditions line up, rather than a guaranteed session.

On the seafront, the Redcar Beacon viewing tower gives you a view up and down the coast, and the Zetland Lifeboat Museum holds one of the oldest surviving lifeboats. For lessons, head to Saltburn, about ten minutes south, where the established surf schools are.

Who it suits

  • Local sessions when the swell lines up
  • Improvers checking several peaks
  • Kitesurfers and windsurfers

Think twice if…

  • Guaranteed surf
  • First lessons (go to Saltburn)
  • Dog walks in the central seafront zone in summer

Surf guide

Surfing Redcar

Redcar faces the North Sea and has several peaks along the seafront. The usual combination is north-east to easterly swell with a south-westerly wind. Low-tide rock scars help shape some of the waves but also make them more hazardous, so check where the rocks are before you paddle out.

The RNLI has recently reported people caught out by the incoming tide here, so keep an eye on the tide as well as the waves. In season, lifeguards patrol a flagged area; outside it, don't surf alone.

FAQs

Redcar: quick answers

Is Redcar lifeguarded?

In 2026, daily 23–31 May, weekends 6 June–12 July, then daily from 18 July to 6 September, 10am–6pm.

Can dogs go on Redcar beach?

Yes, except in the central exclusion zone from The Regent cinema to Graffenberg Street between 1 May and 30 September.

Can I learn to surf at Redcar?

Saltburn, about ten minutes away, has the established surf schools.
