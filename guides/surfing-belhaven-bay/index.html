Scotland beach guide

Belhaven Bay at a glance

Belhaven Bay is the long, open beach on the western edge of Dunbar, and it sits inside John Muir Country Park. Low sand dunes shelter the back of the beach, with salt marsh and grassland behind them, and you look out across the mouth of the Firth of Forth. It feels much wilder than you'd expect for somewhere so close to a town, and it's a lovely spot to walk even when there's no surf at all.

The bay's best-known landmark is the Bridge to Nowhere, a footbridge over the Biel Water. At low tide you can walk over it and out onto the sand. As the tide comes in, the channel fills around it and the bridge ends up standing on its own in the water. Check the tide times before you cross, or you may find you have to wade back or wait it out.

For surfing, Belhaven is one of the gentlest and most forgiving beaches in south-east Scotland. A surf centre by the car park has showers and changing rooms, and Dunbar's cafés and railway station are close by. This is a North Sea beach, though, so it only works when the swell arrives from the north-east or east. Flat spells can last a long time, so keep an eye on the forecast before you make the trip.

Who it suits

  • First lessons and family surf days
  • Longboarders and improvers on gentle waves
  • Car-free trips by train to Dunbar
  • Walkers who want a surf check and a coastal stroll in one trip

Think twice if…

  • Powerful, high-performance surf
  • Anyone needing waves during long North Sea flat spells
  • Swimmers looking for a lifeguarded beach

Surf guide

Surfing Belhaven Bay

Belhaven needs swell from the north-east or east. Winds from the west or south-west blow offshore here, while strong easterlies leave it messy. Most of the bay is sand, with some rock towards the edges. The Biel Water estuary moves the sandbanks around, so the best spot can change from one visit to the next.

No single state of the tide is reliably best, because the banks decide where the waves break. What changes most is how far you have to walk. At low tide the water can be a long way out across the flats, and when the tide turns it fills the channels quickly.

Level

Beginners to intermediates

Forgiving on most days. Intermediates will have more fun when a bigger swell arrives.

Hazards

Fast tides and channels

The incoming tide floods the flats and the estuary channels quickly. Check the tide times before you head far out.

Season

Winter and spring

These are the most dependable months for swell on this stretch of the North Sea.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Shore Road car park

The car park is off Shore Road in Belhaven and has 120 spaces, including a disabled bay and motorhome bays. You pay and display or use the Ringo app, and a day ticket covers East Lothian's other coastal car parks that day. A height barrier restricts taller vehicles, and overnight parking isn't allowed.

Facilities

Toilets, showers, bike racks

There are toilets by the car park, including accessible ones. The surf centre has showers, there are bike racks, and a food outlet opens in season.

Lifeguards

None

East Lothian has no lifeguarded beaches. In a coastal emergency, call 999 or 112 and ask for the Coastguard.

Getting there

Train, bus or A1087

Dunbar station has ScotRail trains from Edinburgh and services from the south, and the bay is about a 15-minute walk away. East Coast Buses also run between Edinburgh and Dunbar. If you're driving, turn off the A1087 near West Barns and follow Shore Road.

Dogs

Country park rules

Dogs are welcome in the country park. Follow any wildlife signs, and keep your dog away from nesting birds.

FAQs

Belhaven Bay: quick answers

Is Belhaven Bay good for beginners?

Yes. It's one of the easiest places in Scotland to learn to surf. There are no lifeguards, though, so if you're new it's best to surf in a lesson.

Where do you park for Belhaven Bay?

Use the council car park on Shore Road. It's pay and display, and your ticket also covers the other East Lothian coastal car parks that day.

Can you get to Belhaven by train?

Yes. Dunbar station is about a 15-minute walk from the bay.

When can you cross the Bridge to Nowhere?

Only around low tide. At high tide the water surrounds it, so check the tide times first.
