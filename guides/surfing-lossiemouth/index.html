Scotland beach guide

Lossiemouth at a glance

Lossiemouth's East Beach is a long strip of sand and dunes cut off from the town by the River Lossie. The dark, peaty river meets the clear water of the Moray Firth right in front of the town, and the best view of it all is from Prospect Terrace above the esplanade.

The beach is reached by a footbridge from the esplanade. The original wooden bridge, built in the 19th-century railway era and used for over a century, failed and closed in 2019, and a community-funded replacement has since been built. Waders such as ringed plover and curlew feed under the bridge.

Behind the beach is Seatown, the old fishing quarter known locally as The Toonie, with its fisher cottages. On the other side of town, West Beach runs out towards Covesea Lighthouse, a quieter, rockier place for sunsets.

Who it suits

  • Beginners and families
  • Intermediates on a north-east swell
  • Weekend trips with a town on hand

Think twice if…

  • Experienced surfers wanting powerful waves
  • Lifeguarded swimming

Surf guide

Surfing Lossiemouth

Surf-forecast describes East Beach as a fairly exposed beach break with lefts and rights, best on a north to north-east swell with a southerly offshore wind. It's surfable on all stages of the tide and the bottom is sand, with no protruding rock.

Winter, especially January, gives the most clean days, though small conditions are common even then. Crowds are rarely a problem. Keep away from the river mouth, where the flow can pull you, and check water-quality advice after heavy rain.

Level

Beginners to intermediates

Sand-bottom waves that are forgiving on most days.

Hazards

River mouth

Stay clear of the River Lossie outflow and the breakwater.

Crowds

Rarely crowded

Even on a swell, the line-up is usually quiet.

FAQs

Lossiemouth: quick answers

What conditions suit Lossiemouth?

A north to north-east swell with a southerly offshore wind. It works on all tides.

How do I get onto East Beach?

Over the footbridge from the esplanade; park at Gregory Place.

Is Lossiemouth good for beginners?

Yes. East Beach has a sandy bottom and is rarely crowded, but there are no lifeguards.

Where's best for sunset?

West Beach, which stretches towards Covesea Lighthouse at low tide.
