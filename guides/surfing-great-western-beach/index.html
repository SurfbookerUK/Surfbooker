Cornwall beach guide

Great Western Beach at a glance

Great Western is one of Newquay's town beaches, tucked below the cliffs a couple of minutes' walk from the town centre. It sits between Towan to the west and Tolcarne to the east. At high tide it feels like a set of small sandy coves backed by high cliffs and rocky inlets, but as the tide drops the sand opens out and joins its neighbours, so you can walk along the shore to Towan or Tolcarne.

Getting down is on foot only. A sloping road leads to the beach but there's no parking on it, and a tarmac path also runs down beside the Great Western Hotel. That makes it one of the easiest Cornish surf beaches to reach without a car, because Newquay station, at the end of the branch line from Par, is close to the town centre. Just be ready for a steep walk back up with a board under your arm.

Low tide is the time for exploring, with caves and rock pools along the foot of the cliffs. Keep an eye on the tide while you do: warning signs around Newquay's beaches mention the risk of being cut off at high tide, along with strong undercurrents, large breaking waves and rockfalls. The RNLI patrols the beach daily through the main summer season, so check this year's dates before you plan a swim or a session.

Who it suits

  • Lessons and first sessions on smaller days
  • Visitors arriving by train or staying in town
  • Families who like rock pools and caves at low tide
  • Dog owners, as dogs are allowed all year

Think twice if…

  • Anyone who finds steep slopes hard, especially carrying kit
  • Drivers wanting to park next to the sand
  • Uncrowded surf on summer days

Surf guide

Surfing Great Western Beach

Great Western sits in the curve of Newquay Bay with the headland sheltering it from the west, so it behaves a lot like Towan: usually smaller and gentler than Fistral. Visit Cornwall describes it as good for novice surfers, and it still picks up the consistent Atlantic swell Newquay is known for. In winter, bigger swells wrap in harder and can make it a punchier, more lively wave.

Because the town beaches join at low tide and separate again as it rises, the shape of the beach and where the waves break change a lot through the day. In summer the water gets very busy with lessons and beginners. Stay in the lifeguarded zones, keep clear of the base of the cliffs, and if you're new, let an instructor choose the spot.

Level

Beginners and improvers

Usually a gentler option than Fistral, and popular for lessons on suitable days.

Conditions

Sheltered from the west

The headland takes the edge off westerly wind and swell; big winter swells can still make it punchy.

Patrol

RNLI in the summer season

The RNLI listed daily 10am–6pm cover from 16 May to 27 September for 2026. Check this year's dates.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Park in town and walk

There's no parking on the sloping access road, so use one of the central Newquay car parks and walk down.

Access

Sloping road or hotel path

Walk down the sloping road or the tarmac path beside the Great Western Hotel. At low tide you can also arrive along the sand from Towan or Tolcarne. Both routes are steep on the way back.

Facilities

Toilets nearby, town above

There are toilets close to the beach, and the town centre's cafés and shops are a couple of minutes' walk away.

Dogs

Allowed all year

Local beach guides list dogs as allowed all year, and Great Western isn't in Cornwall Council's restrictions table. Check signs on the day.

By train

Newquay station

Newquay is the end of the Atlantic Coast Line from Par, and the station is close to the town centre and its beaches.

FAQs

Great Western Beach: quick answers

Can you park at Great Western Beach?

No. The sloping road down to the beach has no parking, so leave the car in a town centre car park and walk.

Is Great Western good for beginners?

Often, yes. It's sheltered from the west and usually gentler than Fistral, and it's popular for lessons. It does get busy in summer, so stay in the lifeguarded zones.

Can you walk from Great Western to Towan and Tolcarne?

Yes, at low tide the sand joins up with both neighbouring beaches. Check tide times first so you don't get cut off as the water comes back in.

Are dogs allowed on Great Western Beach?

Yes, local beach guides list dogs as allowed all year, and it isn't on Cornwall Council's restrictions list. Check signs when you arrive.
