Croyde at a glance

A small village with a serious surf reputation.

Croyde sits on the west-facing North Devon coast between Saunton Sands and Baggy Point. Its compact sandy bay, dunes and traditional village make it one of North Devon's most distinctive surf destinations.

The beach can produce fast, hollow and powerful waves, particularly as the tide drops. Smaller summer conditions can be much more approachable, but tide, swell, wind and crowds can completely change the experience.

Off the beach, Croyde has pubs, cafes, surf businesses, accommodation and the South West Coast Path all close by. For a more forgiving beach day nearby, Woolacombe and Saunton Sands are both worth a look.

Before you paddle out

Croyde can have strong rip currents, powerful low-tide surf and heavy crowds. Check the tide, swell and wind, and use the lifeguarded area when patrols are operating. New surfers are better off taking a lesson.

Beach & surf guide

Know Croyde before you paddle out.

A compact, powerful beach break

Croyde is a west-facing sandy bay framed by Down End to the south and Baggy Point to the north. It produces left and right-hand waves and can become fast, hollow and powerful as the tide drops.

Mid tide is often more manageable. Low tide can produce heavy barrels, while high tide can bring a strong shore break.

Swell

West to south-west

Croyde is highly exposed to Atlantic swell, with west and south-westerly swell particularly relevant.

Wind

East is offshore

East to south-east winds are generally favourable for cleaner surf.

Crowds

Expect company

Croyde can become extremely busy. Good surf etiquette matters.

Surfbooker local tips

The useful bits before you go.

Don't treat every tide the same

Mid tide is often the more manageable window. Low tide can become steeper, hollower and more powerful, while high tide can bring a heavy shore break.

Easterly winds are the ones to watch

East to south-east winds are generally favourable offshore directions. Westerlies can quickly make the bay messy.

New to surfing? Consider a lesson

Croyde can work for learning when small, but its rips, crowds and punchier waves make local instruction worthwhile. Saunton Sands is often more forgiving.

Walk Baggy Point

The National Trust coastline around Baggy Point gives excellent views back over Croyde and along the North Devon coast.

Bringing a dog?

Dogs are restricted from Croyde Beach during the main summer period, given here as 1 May to 30 September. Check current signs.

If Croyde is too much, move

Saunton is generally more forgiving, while Putsborough can offer useful shelter from southerly and south-westerly winds.

Safety

Dogs

Summer restriction

Dogs

Dogs on the beach.

Dogs are not permitted on Croyde Beach from 1 May to 30 September. They are allowed from 1 October to 30 April.

The restriction applies to the beach during the summer season, so check the current signs if you are visiting with a dog.

Beach dog restrictions can change

Beach dog restrictions can change. Check the signs at the beach when you arrive, particularly around the start and end of the summer season.

Did you know?

Croyde has more history than its surf reputation suggests.

Domesday Croyde

Croyde appears in the Domesday Book as Crideholde or Crideholda.

Wartime training

American troops trained in this part of North Devon ahead of the D-Day landings.

Oceanfest

Croyde has hosted its annual surf and music festival since 1999.

Protected coast

National Trust-owned Baggy Point sits immediately beside the bay.

FAQs

Quick answers before you go.

Is Croyde good for beginner surfers?

It can be on small suitable days, especially with a surf school, but strong rips, powerful low-tide waves and crowds can make it challenging. Saunton is often more forgiving.

How many surf schools are there in Croyde?

Surfbooker currently includes Surf South West, Surfing Croyde Bay and Lords of Down End.

Does Walking on Waves teach at Croyde?

Walking on Waves has a dedicated Croyde surf-hire location, but its lessons operate from Saunton Sands.

What tide is best?

Mid tide is often more manageable. Low tide can be steep, hollow and powerful, while high tide can bring a heavy shore break.

What wind is best?

East to south-east winds are generally favourable offshore directions.

Is Croyde crowded?

Often, yes. It is one of the UK's best-known surf beaches and the compact lineup can become very busy.
