Cayton Bay at a glance

Cayton Bay is a cliff-backed bay roughly four miles south of Scarborough, one of the string of sandy bays along this stretch of coast between Scarborough and Filey. The council describes it as a small sandy bay backed by cliffs, with a mix of sand and rock, and it's a long-standing favourite with surfers and with visitors from the holiday park above.

The cliffs here are on the move. A major landslip in April 2008 near Osgodby left bungalows on the Knipe Point estate at the cliff edge, and the slope and the National Trust land below it are a Site of Special Scientific Interest. Look out too for the Second World War pillboxes, many of them damaged by coastal erosion.

The National Park describes Cayton as a place for more powerful breaks for experienced surfers, especially in winter and spring, and as one of the few spots around here that can be surfed at both low and high tide. A surf school and shop operate from the clifftop car park all year. Beginners are best off booking a lesson rather than going it alone.

Who it suits

  • Experienced surfers looking for more power than the town beaches
  • Improvers taking lessons with the clifftop school
  • Surfers who want options across the tide
  • People happy with a steep walk down

Think twice if…

  • Wheelchair users or anyone who struggles with steep paths
  • Beginners surfing without an instructor

Surf guide

Surfing Cayton Bay

Cayton has several distinct breaks rather than one. Surf-forecast lists a sandbar break at the Bunkers, which it says is best around high tide, and a separate point break, both working best on a north swell; it gives south-westerly winds as best at the Bunkers and westerly at the Point. That variety is why the National Park says the bay can be surfed at low and high tide.

This is where Scarborough's surfers come when they want more push. Surf-forecast warns about rocks, rips and pollution, and expects crowds at the Bunkers when there's swell, while the Point is usually quieter. If you're still learning, book a lesson rather than paddling out alone.

Level

Improvers to experienced

More powerful than Scarborough's bays; beginners should surf with an instructor.

Conditions

North swell, westerly winds

Surf-forecast rates north swell best; SW wind at the Bunkers, W at the Point.

Hazards

Rocks and rips

Surf-forecast flags rocks, rips and pollution at both breaks.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Lifeguards

RNLI, 10am–6pm

In 2026 RNLI lifeguards patrolled from 2 May, at weekends through early May and then daily until 6 September.

Parking

Clifftop car park

Cayton Bay beach car park is at Killerby Cliff (YO11 3NR). It's a grass field that can be tricky after heavy rain.

Access

Steep path down

The path from the car park to the beach is steep and not suitable for wheelchairs or people with poor balance.

Facilities

By the car park

Toilets and a surf shop sit next to the clifftop car park; the council also lists a café and shop.

Dogs

Allowed

The council lists dogs as allowed, and Cayton isn't one of the zones in the Scarborough-area seasonal beach ban.

FAQs

Cayton Bay: quick answers

Is Cayton Bay good for beginners?

Only with an instructor. The National Park describes Cayton's breaks as more powerful and aimed at experienced surfers; Scarborough's bays are gentler for learning on your own.

What tide is best at Cayton Bay?

It depends on the break. The National Park says Cayton can be surfed at both low and high tide, and surf-forecast rates the Bunkers best around high tide.

Can you take dogs to Cayton Bay?

Yes. The council lists dogs as allowed, and the bay isn't covered by the summer beach bans in the Scarborough-area dog control order.

Is the beach accessible?

Not easily. The path down from the clifftop car park is steep and unsuitable for wheelchairs.
