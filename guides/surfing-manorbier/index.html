Wales beach guide

Manorbier at a glance

Manorbier sits between Tenby and Pembroke, and its bay is overlooked by a medieval castle. Gerald of Wales, who was born there in the 12th century, called it the pleasantest spot in Wales. The beach mixes sand, rock and pebbles beneath cliffs and dunes. The 12th-century Church of St James watches from the opposite slope, and the Neolithic chambered tomb of King's Quoit sits on the headland above.

It faces south-west, which makes it one of the more reliable surf beaches on the south Pembrokeshire coast and a long-standing local favourite. There are no lifeguards, and the water can have strong currents, so it is better suited to people who already surf. At low tide there is good rock pooling. In summer it gets very busy, so arrive early if you want to get in the water.

The beach has excellent water quality, a Green Coast Award and Marine Conservation Society recommendation. The coast path runs east from here to Tenby and west towards Freshwater East.

Who it suits

  • Improvers and intermediate surfers
  • Families who like rock pooling and a castle in the background
  • Walkers on the coast path towards Tenby or Freshwater East

Think twice if…

  • Learners without an instructor — there are no lifeguards
  • Late arrivals on busy summer days

Surf guide

Surfing Manorbier

Manorbier faces south-west and picks up the westerly swells that run along the south Pembrokeshire coast. There is a beach break over sand, with rocky reef sections that can give better-shaped waves at the right stage of tide. Northerly winds are offshore.

Rocks show around low tide, and the incoming tide can cut off parts of the beach, so check tide times before you go. Visit Pembrokeshire notes strong currents. With no lifeguards, never surf alone here.

Level

Improvers upwards

Rocky sections and no lifeguards make it best for surfers with some experience.

Conditions

South-west swell, northerly wind

Reef sections and rocks change the beach a lot with the tide.

Hazards

Rocks, currents and tide

Take care over rocks, watch for strong currents and keep an eye on the incoming tide.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

National Park car park

A pay-and-display car park run by the National Park Authority, free outside the main season, with disabled spaces. Postcode SA70 8QR.

Facilities

Toilets

Toilets including accessible facilities. Cafés and a pub are nearby in the village.

Access

Level path and viewing platform

A level path leads to a beach-head platform with seating. The last 40 metres include three short slopes of up to 1:10. A hearing loop is available.

Lifeguards

None

There is no lifeguard service. Avoid rough or unfamiliar conditions.

Dogs

Allowed

Dogs are allowed on the beach.

FAQs

Manorbier: quick answers

Is Manorbier good for surfing?

Yes, for surfers with some experience. It faces south-west and picks up westerly swell, but it has rocks and no lifeguards.

Are dogs allowed on Manorbier beach?

Yes, dogs are allowed.

Is Manorbier beach accessible?

There is a level path from the car park to a beach-head viewing platform with seating, with a few short slopes near the end.

What is King's Quoit?

A Neolithic chambered tomb above the bay.
