Cornwall beach guide

Gwithian & Godrevy at a glance

Gwithian and Godrevy share the north-eastern end of St Ives Bay, where around three miles of sand runs from the Hayle river mouth to the Red River at Godrevy. The Red River gets its name from the mining waste that once stained its water. Beyond it is Godrevy Point, with the lighthouse on Godrevy Island just offshore. First lit in 1859 to mark the Stones reef, it's the light Virginia Woolf visited in 1892 and which is said to have inspired To the Lighthouse.

At low tide there's a huge amount of sand, with big areas of rock pools and some caves to explore. Gwithian and Godrevy are really opposite ends of the same beach, but they have separate car parks, cafés and walks, so pick your end before you set off. The dunes behind, Gwithian Towans, are protected for wildlife and cover a long history, including an excavated Bronze Age farm and an early chapel that the shifting sand uncovered and then buried again.

This is one of west Cornwall's most consistent surf zones, and it gets busy with lessons in summer. The waves generally get bigger the closer you are to Godrevy, so you can often find something to suit your level just by walking along the beach. RNLI lifeguards patrol in season. Grey seals haul out in Mutton Cove below the headland; watch them from the clifftop and never go down to them.

Who it suits

  • Learners and improvers on lifeguarded days
  • Surfers wanting consistent swell in west Cornwall
  • Wildlife watchers and clifftop walkers
  • Families who like rock pools at low tide

Think twice if…

  • Dog walkers on the beach from 10am to 6pm in July and August
  • Anyone wanting a sheltered spot when the swell is big

Surf guide

Surfing Gwithian & Godrevy

Gwithian faces into St Ives Bay and picks up most north-coast swell, which is why it's so consistent. On a good day it gives long, walling lefts and rights, with the chance of something hollower around low tide. Size builds towards Godrevy, and so does the influence of rock near the headland.

The classic recipe is a medium-sized north-coast swell with a southerly or easterly wind; south-east is straight offshore. The beach is very open, so wind direction makes a big difference. Peaks move with the sandbanks, so take time to watch before you paddle out.

Level

All levels

Smaller waves towards Gwithian, bigger towards Godrevy. Walk the beach to find what suits you.

Wind

South-easterly offshore

Southerly to easterly winds clean it up; it's very exposed to anything onshore.

Hazards

Rocks towards Godrevy

Rock reefs, pools and caves near the headland. Stay in the flagged areas when lifeguards are on duty.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Gwithian or National Trust Godrevy

At Gwithian there's parking between the road and the beach, with a path through the dunes to the sand. At Godrevy, the National Trust runs a year-round car park with limited spaces, an overflow field in warmer months (weather and ground permitting) and a small area on the way up to the headland. National Trust members park free but need to register.

Facilities

Cafés at both ends

There's a café and beach shop by Gwithian's main car park. At Godrevy, the café (not run by the National Trust) sits near the beach, and toilets, including an accessible one, are in the field car park on the headland.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

Swim between the red and yellow flags and surf between the black and white flags. Check the RNLI website for this year's dates.

Dogs

July and August daytime ban

Cornwall Council prohibits dogs on the beach from Harveys Towans to Godrevy Point from 10am to 6pm, 1 July to 31 August.

Getting there

B3301, bus or train

Godrevy is just off the B3301 north of Gwithian village. Hayle station is about five miles away, and buses stop in Gwithian, from where it's a quarter of a mile to a mile on foot depending on where you start.

FAQs

Gwithian & Godrevy: quick answers

Can I see seals at Godrevy?

Yes, grey seals often haul out in Mutton Cove below the headland. Watch from the clifftop, keep your distance and keep dogs on leads.

Should I go to Gwithian or Godrevy?

It's the same stretch of sand, but the access is different. Gwithian is handy for the village cafés and dune paths; Godrevy has National Trust parking, the headland walks and the lighthouse views. Waves are usually a little bigger at the Godrevy end.

Can dogs go on the beach?

Yes, apart from 10am to 6pm between 1 July and 31 August, when Cornwall Council bans dogs from Harveys Towans to Godrevy Point.

Can I walk to Hayle along the beach?

The sand runs for about three miles from the Red River to the Hayle river mouth. Keep an eye on the tide, and stay well away from the estuary channel at the Hayle end.
