Wales beach guide

Whitesands at a glance

Whitesands Bay, or Porth Mawr – 'big harbour' in Welsh – is the main sandy beach on the St Davids peninsula, right out at the western tip of Pembrokeshire. The rocky hill of Carn Llidi rises behind it, and the coast runs north from the sand towards St Davids Head. Looking out to sea, you see Ramsey Island and the Bishops and Clerks, a scatter of reefs and islets with a long record of shipwrecks.

It is one of the best-known surf beaches on the Welsh coast and works as a full summer beach. A car park sits behind the sand, with a café, shop, toilets, a slipway and a first aid point, plus surfboard hire and lessons in season. Lifeguards patrol over the summer months, which makes it a sensible choice if you are learning or bringing a family. The trade-off is popularity: in the school holidays the car park can fill by late morning.

There is history here too. At the northern end of the beach are the scant remains of a chapel dedicated to St Patrick. Sailors are said to have prayed there before setting out and given thanks on a safe return. Outside summer the bay is far quieter, and with the coast path to St Davids Head starting from the sand, a surf and a walk fit easily into one day.

Who it suits

  • First lessons and improvers on small to moderate days
  • Families who want lifeguards, a café and toilets in one place
  • Walkers heading for St Davids Head and Carn Llidi
  • Anyone staying in or around St Davids

Think twice if…

  • Dog walkers from 1 May to 30 September, when there is a total ban
  • Late arrivals on summer holiday days, as the car park fills

Surf guide

Surfing Whitesands

A sandy bay break that is more sheltered than it looks on a map.

Whitesands faces west towards Ramsey Island and picks up Atlantic swell from the west and south-west. The headlands and offshore islands give it some shelter, so on the same swell it often has smaller, more manageable waves than more exposed beaches such as Newgale. The north end, near the slipway and rocks, and the middle of the bay can have different sandbanks, so watch for a few minutes before you paddle out.

Light easterly winds blow offshore and tidy the waves up. The beach narrows noticeably as the tide comes in, which pushes everyone together and brings the rocks at each end into play. On bigger days rip currents develop, so the beach suits experienced surfers in winter swells and learners on gentler summer days with lifeguards on duty.

Level

Beginner to intermediate

A good learning beach in summer, with lessons and lifeguards. Bigger winter swells are for experienced surfers.

Conditions

West/south-west swell, easterly wind

Often smaller than exposed beaches nearby. Check the tide, as the beach shrinks at high water.

Hazards

Rocks, rips and crowds

Rocky edges at both ends and rips on bigger days. In summer, lessons, bodyboarders and swimmers all share the water.

Ma Simes Surf School & hire

Surf school & hire
masimes.co.uk

Two-hour beginner and improver lessons and private coaching at Whitesands, run by ASI/ISA instructors who are also qualified beach lifeguards. Minimum age 8 and able to swim 50 m. Surfboard and wetsuit hire on the beach must be pre-booked; the shop is at 28 High Street, St Davids.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Pay car park behind the beach

Spaces fill up in summer, so arrive early in the holidays or use the seasonal shuttle from St Davids.

Facilities

Café, shop and toilets

Toilets including accessible facilities, a café, a shop, a slipway and a first aid point.

Lifeguards

Summer patrols

Lifeguards patrol during the summer months. Swim between the red and yellow flags and surf between the black and white flags. Check RNLI dates before you go.

Dogs

Total summer ban

Pembrokeshire County Council bans dogs from Whitesands from 1 May to 30 September. Leisure staff enforce the ban.

Water quality

Blue Flag beach

Whitesands is listed as a Blue Flag beach.

Accommodation

Where to stay near Whitesands

St Davids has the widest choice; there are also campsites and holiday parks on the peninsula.

City

St Davids

Hotels, B&Bs, pubs and restaurants about 2 miles away. The best base if you want evenings out.

Close to the sand

Peninsula campsites and cottages

Several camping and caravan sites and holiday cottages sit between St Davids and the beach — handy for dawn surfs.

Alternative

Solva or Newgale

Twenty minutes east, with Newgale's more exposed surf as a second option.

Things to do around Whitesands

The medieval cathedral and Bishop's Palace in Britain's smallest city.

Boat trips around Ramsey Island run from St Justinian's in season — seals, seabirds and tidal races.

Travel

Getting to Whitesands

By car

From St Davids

Follow the B4583 west out of St Davids; the beach is about 2 miles away. Lanes are narrow.

By bus

Celtic Coaster

A seasonal shuttle links St Davids with Whitesands and St Justinian's. Check current Pembrokeshire coastal bus timetables.

By train

Haverfordwest or Fishguard

The nearest stations are Haverfordwest and Fishguard; the T11 bus runs to St Davids.

FAQs

Whitesands: quick answers

Can dogs go on Whitesands beach?

Not in summer. The council has a total dog ban from 1 May to 30 September. Outside those dates, dogs are allowed.

Is Whitesands good for beginners?

Yes, on small to moderate days, ideally with a lesson and when lifeguards are on duty. Watch out for the rocks at each end as the tide rises.

What does Porth Mawr mean?

It is the Welsh name for the bay and means 'big harbour'.

How early should I arrive to park?

In the summer holidays the car park can fill by late morning, so arrive early.
