Wales beach guide

Port Eynon at a glance

Port Eynon is a small village at the end of the road on the south Gower, where a sandy bay curves east to the neighbouring village of Horton about half a mile away. Behind the beach are a pay car park, toilets, cafés, a campsite and a pub, and a slipway is used to launch dive and sailing boats. The village is small, so in summer the car park is the easiest way in.

It is a classic family beach, with a wide stretch of sand and rock pools to explore at low tide, and RNLI lifeguards in the summer holidays. The village sits inside the Gower's Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty and on the Wales Coast Path. Dogs are allowed all year on the western part of the beach, from the main steps to the Salt House, but are banned east of the steps from May to September. Swim between the red and yellow flags when the lifeguards are on.

There is plenty of history. A lifeboat ran from here until 1919; three young crewmen died in 1916 going to the SS Dunvegan, and their memorial is in the churchyard. Along the point are the ruined Salt House and the old dovecote of Culver Hole. Culver Hole is a Grade II listed scheduled monument, and the youth hostel at the south end of the bay is a converted lifeboat house.

Who it suits

  • Families
  • Beginners and longboarders on small days
  • Divers, kayakers and sailors

Surf guide

Surfing Port Eynon

Port Eynon faces south and needs a solid south-westerly swell to break. When it works the waves are gentle and good for learners and longboarders, and when Llangennith is too big it can be the sensible choice.

Lower tides open up more sand and the rock pools at the edges; keep clear of the rocks and, in season, stay between the red and yellow flags.

Level

Learners and longboarders

Gentle waves when there is swell, with lifeguards in the summer holidays.

Conditions

Solid south-west swell

Often flat; a fallback when the west coast is too big.

Hazards

Rocks and patrol gaps

Rocks at both ends, and lifeguards only on set dates — no flags means no lifeguards.

Lifeguards

Summer holidays

In 2026 the RNLI patrolled 23–31 May, weekends 6–28 June, and daily 4 July–6 September, 10am–6pm.

Dogs

Part-beach summer ban

Dogs are allowed all year from the main steps west to the Salt House. From the steps east to the Horton beach access they are banned 1 May–30 September.

FAQs

Port Eynon: quick answers

Are there lifeguards at Port Eynon?

Yes, in season. In 2026 that was 23–31 May, weekends in June, and daily from 4 July to 6 September, 10am–6pm.

Can I take my dog to Port Eynon?

Yes, west of the main steps towards the Salt House all year. East of the steps to Horton, dogs are banned from 1 May to 30 September.

Can I walk to Horton?

Yes, Horton is about half a mile along the beach to the east.

Is Port Eynon good for surfing?

Only with a solid south-west swell, when it suits learners and longboarders.
