Southern England beach guide

Bracklesham Bay at a glance

Bracklesham sits in the middle of Bracklesham Bay, the long stretch of Channel coast on the Manhood Peninsula south-west of Chichester. East Wittering runs on to the west and Selsey sits at the south-eastern tip of the bay. The beach is shingle at the top, with sand showing as the tide goes out, and on a clear day you can see the Isle of Wight across the water. The seafront car park at the end of Bracklesham Lane puts you right on the beach.

Bracklesham is best known for what washes out of the foreshore. The Eocene beds here have produced more than 160 species of fossil fish, and on a falling tide you can find shells and small shark and ray teeth on the wet sand and at the foot of the shingle, working east towards Earnley. The bay is an SSSI, so pick up only loose material, don't dig into the beds, and leave plenty of time to get back before the tide turns.

The coastline itself keeps changing. East Thorney, once part of the parish, now lies under the sea off Bracklesham, and in 2013 the Environment Agency deliberately breached the shingle bank at Medmerry, to the east, creating an RSPB nature reserve as part of a major flood-defence scheme. There are no lifeguards along this stretch, though Chichester District Council describes the beach as gently sloping with no rip currents.

Surf guide

Surfing Bracklesham Bay

Bracklesham is a Channel beach break that needs a south-westerly swell to come alive, which usually means autumn and winter storms rather than summer. Most swell here is short-period windswell, so the best sessions tend to come when a swell holds as the wind drops or swings to the north or north-east, which is offshore along this stretch.

The council says the beach shelves gently and has no rip currents, but waves can get big at high tide and make it hard to get back out of the water over the shingle. Kitesurfers and windsurfers use the bay too, so give them room, and remember there's no lifeguard cover. If in doubt, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.

Season

Autumn and winter

Channel swell is most likely from autumn to early spring. Summer is usually small and much busier.

Hazards

High-tide shingle

Bigger waves at high tide can make it tricky to get out of the water up the shingle. Watch for other water users.

Level

Improvers on the right day

The gentle slope helps, but with no lifeguards and inconsistent swell it suits surfers who can judge conditions.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Bracklesham Lane seafront

The council's pay car park at the end of Bracklesham Lane is right on the beach, with disabled spaces. It often fills on fine days, so arrive early.

Access

Shingle and a concrete apron

The shingle is hard going for wheelchairs, but the concrete apron at Bracklesham is accessible. Public toilets are close to the car park.

Lifeguards

None

There are no lifeguards here; the nearest patrolled beach in high season is West Wittering. The council's foreshore officers work from Easter to the end of September. In an emergency, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.

Dogs

Allowed all year

The council says well-behaved dogs can go anywhere on Bracklesham beach; pick up after them. The district's dog control order is being renewed in autumn 2026, so check for changes.

By bus

Stagecoach 52 from Chichester

The 52 runs between Chichester and the Witterings and stops on Bracklesham Lane. Chichester has the nearest railway station; check current timetables.

FAQs

Bracklesham Bay: quick answers

Can you surf Bracklesham Bay?

Yes, mainly in autumn and winter when a south-westerly swell pushes up the Channel. It's inconsistent, so check the forecast and the beach before you go.

Where can you find fossils at Bracklesham?

Start on the foreshore near the Bracklesham Lane car park on a falling tide and work east towards Earnley, checking the wet sand and the foot of the shingle. Collect only loose finds, as the site is an SSSI.

Are dogs allowed on Bracklesham beach?

Yes. Chichester District Council allows well-behaved dogs on the whole beach all year, as long as you clear up after them. Check for any changes under the council's new dog control order.

Is Bracklesham beach lifeguarded?

No. For a lifeguarded beach in high season, the council points visitors to West Wittering.
