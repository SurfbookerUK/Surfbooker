Cornwall beach guide

Trevaunance Cove at a glance

Trevaunance Cove is St Agnes' beach: a small bay of sand and shingle at the bottom of Trevaunance Coombe, the steep valley that drops from the village to the sea. A headland gives it some shelter from the west, but it's open to the north and north-east. A pub, beach shop, café and toilets are all within a minute or two of the sand, while the village centre is about a kilometre uphill on steep roads with no pavement.

The cove has a long history as a harbour. The Tonkin family tried to build one here from the 1630s, and storms kept wrecking their efforts; a version involving Henry Winstanley, the engineer of the first Eddystone Lighthouse, was destroyed in 1705. A later stone pier served the St Agnes mines through the 18th and 19th centuries, and at low tide you can still see the remains of the old breakwater on the western side of the beach.

Low tide also uncovers rocks and rock pools, and you can walk along the shore to Trevellas Porth. Keep a close eye on the tide if you do, because if you get caught out the only way back is a very steep climb to the coast path. The cliffs are a geological SSSI with mineral veins showing in the rock, and the cove sits in the St Agnes Mining District of the Cornish Mining World Heritage Site.

Who it suits

  • Improvers and confident surfers on clean mid-tide days
  • Surf trips with a village and pub to come back to
  • Rock pooling and low-tide exploring
  • Mining history and coast path walks

Think twice if…

  • Easy access with lots of kit or a buggy
  • Reliable summer surf, as it's often flat
  • Arriving by bus to the beach itself

Surf guide

Surfing Trevaunance Cove

Spot guides describe St Agnes as a fairly exposed beach break over sand with rocks around it, mostly producing rights and usually at its best around mid tide. Winds from the south and south-south-east are offshore. It picks up both local windswell and longer-range groundswell, but summer is often flat, so autumn to spring is your best chance of good waves.

When it's working it gets crowded, so be patient and follow surf etiquette. Rocks are the main hazard, and local safety guides also list strong currents, which build when the swell is bigger. Lifeguards only cover the cove in summer, so check RNLI dates and the flags on arrival. Outside patrol times it's better suited to surfers who can read a small rocky cove.

Level

Improvers and above

Small days can suit improvers. Bigger swells, rocks and currents make it one for confident surfers.

Hazards

Rocks, currents and tide

Avoid the rocks at the edges, watch for currents in bigger swell, and don't get cut off walking to Trevellas Porth.

Conditions

Mid tide, southerly wind

Usually best around mid tide with winds from the south or south-south-east. Often flat in summer.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Three small car parks

There are two private car parks near the beach and a council car park further up the approach road, which has a very steep entrance. At busy times, park legally in St Agnes and walk down.

Access

Steep in every direction

The road down to the cove is very steep, as is the slipway by the old harbour. The walk back up to the village is about a kilometre on steep lanes with no pavement.

Facilities

Pub, café and toilets

Public toilets, a pub, a beach shop and a café are all a minute or two from the sand. Seats along the road give you somewhere to rest on the climb.

Lifeguards

Summer only

The cove has RNLI lifeguards in summer. Check the RNLI website for this year's dates and follow the flags.

Getting there

A30, then the B3277

Leave the A30 at Chiverton Cross and follow the B3277 for about four miles into St Agnes, then follow signs to the cove. There's no public transport to the cove itself, so bus users will need to walk down from the village.

FAQs

Trevaunance Cove: quick answers

Is there parking at Trevaunance Cove?

Yes, but not much. There are two private car parks near the beach and a council car park further up the hill. On busy days, park legally in St Agnes and walk down.

Can you walk from Trevaunance Cove to Trevellas Porth?

At low tide you can walk along the shore. Watch the tide carefully, because if you get cut off the way back is a very steep climb to the coast path.

When is the surf best at Trevaunance Cove?

Usually around mid tide, with a southerly or south-south-easterly wind. It's often flat in summer, so autumn to spring is more reliable.

Can dogs go on Trevaunance Cove?

The cove isn't listed in Cornwall Council's dog restrictions table. Other guides mention lead rules in summer, so check the signs when you arrive.
