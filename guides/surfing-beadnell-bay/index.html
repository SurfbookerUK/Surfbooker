Northeast England & Yorkshire beach guide

Beadnell Bay at a glance

Beadnell Bay is a horseshoe of golden sand that runs south for miles from the village harbour, backed by big dunes. The Good Beach Guide has recommended it for the highest UK bathing water quality, and the long, open shape gives you plenty of room even when the village is busy.

The harbour at the north end is said to be the only west-facing harbour on England's east coast. Next to it stand 18th-century lime kilns, and the village's Craster Arms dates back to the 15th century, built around a three-storey stone pele tower. Beadnell Sailing Club has been here for more than 40 years.

This is one of Northumberland's best-equipped watersports bays. Alongside surfing and bodyboarding, people windsurf, kitesurf, sail, dive and snorkel here, and the Farne Islands are a popular sailing trip. If you want a walk afterwards, follow the dunes south to Low Newton.

Surf guide

Surfing Beadnell Bay

The north end of the bay is sheltered by the harbour, so the surf is usually best in the middle and south of the bay. Beadnell faces the North Sea and needs north-easterly to easterly swell, and westerly winds blow offshore. On a good swell, local guides say the waves can reach 15ft, which is no place for learners.

Because the bay is so popular with kitesurfers and windsurfers, you'll often share the water. Give them space, especially on windy days. There's no lifeguard cover, so check the forecast and the beach before you paddle out.

FAQs

Beadnell Bay: quick answers

Can you surf at Beadnell Bay?

Yes, on a north-east to east swell with westerly winds. The middle and south of the bay are usually best.

Is Beadnell lifeguarded?

No.

What other watersports happen at Beadnell?

Windsurfing, kitesurfing, sailing, diving and snorkelling are all popular, and there's a sailing club in the village.
