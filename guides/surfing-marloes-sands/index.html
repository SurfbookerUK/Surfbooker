Wales beach guide

Marloes Sands at a glance

Marloes Sands is a long beach of more than a mile on the Marloes peninsula, near the south-western tip of Wales, owned by the National Trust and backed by high cliffs. Layered sandstone and shale fins run out across the sand. The tidal island of Gateholm sits at the western end, with Skokholm out on the horizon.

You reach it on foot from the National Trust car park, about half a mile away, which helps it feel remote even in summer. There are toilets at the car park, but nothing on the beach itself. Timing matters too: the beach is covered at high tide, so plan your visit around low water, when the rocks and pools are fully exposed.

The peninsula is a big draw for wildlife. Boats to Skomer and Skokholm, with their puffins and Manx shearwaters, leave from nearby Martin's Haven. Grey seals pup around the Deer Park in late summer. Gateholm has remains of prehistoric settlement and was dug by Time Team.

Who it suits

  • Walkers on the Marloes peninsula circuit
  • Photographers and geology fans
  • Experienced surfers happy with a remote, unpatrolled beach
  • Wildlife watchers

Think twice if…

  • Anyone who needs facilities close to the sand
  • Visitors arriving at high tide, when the beach is covered

Surf guide

Surfing Marloes Sands

Marloes is fully exposed to the open ocean and picks up south-westerly swell, so it can have good waves. But it is rocky, remote and unpatrolled, and help is a long walk away.

The rock fins and the tide shape everything here. At low tide the sand opens up into a long stretch broken by outcrops; at high tide the sea reaches the cliffs. Surf only if you are experienced and with others, and check tide times first.

Level

Experienced only

Remote, rocky and unpatrolled — not for learners.

Conditions

South-west swell, low to mid tide

The beach is covered at high tide, so plan around low water.

Hazards

Rocks, tide and remoteness

Rock fins cross the beach and the tide comes up to the cliffs. Further west, Jack Sound has tidal rips.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Marloes Sands car park

A National Trust car park about half a mile from the beach.

Facilities

Toilets at the car park

Toilets at the Marloes Sands and Martin's Haven car parks. Nothing on the beach; more in Marloes village.

Lifeguards

None

There is no lifeguard service.

Dogs

Allowed, no bins

Dogs are allowed, but there are no dog bins, so take waste home. Keep dogs on leads near livestock and on the coast path.

Gateholm

Tidal island

Reachable on foot only at low tide, and the climb is for the sure-footed only.

FAQs

Marloes Sands: quick answers

Is Marloes Sands covered at high tide?

Yes. The National Trust notes that the beach is covered at high tide, so check tide times.

How far is the walk from the car park?

About half a mile from the National Trust car park.

Can dogs go on Marloes Sands?

Yes, but there are no dog bins. Keep dogs on leads around livestock and on the coast path.

Can you walk to Gateholm?

Only at low tide, and the climb onto the island is tricky.
