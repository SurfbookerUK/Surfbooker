Southern England beach guide

Shanklin at a glance

Shanklin's beach runs along the foot of the cliffs on the Isle of Wight's east coast, with a pedestrian esplanade, gardens and hotels behind it. The sand is mostly fine, with an area of shingle towards the southern end. Above the seafront sit the town centre and the thatched Old Village, linked to the beach by a public cliff lift. It's a traditional family resort beach, well set up for a day by the sea rather than a surf mission.

Surf here is occasional and small. The island's east coast faces away from the prevailing Channel swell, so waves tend to be gentle when they show at all. Wightlink lists neighbouring Sandown, which shares the same long bay, as a place for gentle rolling waves on small swells, ideal for first-timers and bodyboarders. If you're after more consistent surf, Compton Bay on the south-west coast is the island's most popular spot.

At the south end of the seafront, Shanklin Chine cuts down 32 metres to sea level through a wooded ravine. It has welcomed visitors since at least 1817, was used by commandos as an assault course before the 1942 Dieppe Raid, and carried part of the PLUTO fuel pipeline to Normandy in 1944. A short stretch of that pipework is still preserved there.

Surf guide

Surfing Shanklin

Shanklin sits on the sheltered east side of the island, so the south-westerly swell that feeds Compton rarely reaches it with much size. When something does arrive, expect small, soft waves over sand, better suited to a bodyboard or a first stand-up than to a performance session.

There's no lifeguard patrol listed for Shanklin in the council's beach information, and the council notes natural hazards and advises reading the safety signs and checking tide tables. Lifebelts are placed along the esplanade and an inshore rescue service is nearby.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Access

Cliff lift, slipways and steps

A public lift links the cliff top and esplanade; several concrete slipways give wheelchair and pushchair access to the sand.

Toilets

Three blocks

By the putting green, behind the Spa car park and at the southern end, with accessible toilets.

Parking

Spa car park and on-street

Three car parks along the seafront including the Spa car park, plus on-street spaces and some disabled bays.

Dogs

Summer ban

Dogs aren't allowed on the beach from May to September inclusive; designated areas apply at other times.

Food

Kiosk and esplanade cafés

A beach kiosk and a café along the esplanade, with hotels and restaurants on the gardens side.

FAQs

Shanklin: quick answers

Can you surf at Shanklin?

Occasionally, and it's usually small and gentle. Compton Bay is the island's most popular surf spot.

Can dogs go on Shanklin beach?

Not from May to September inclusive. Outside those months, check the designated areas on the council's dog control orders.

Is there step-free access to Shanklin beach?

Yes. The cliff lift links the town to the esplanade, and concrete slipways lead onto the sand.

Is Shanklin beach lifeguarded?

The council's beach information doesn't list lifeguards. There are lifebelts along the esplanade and an inshore rescue service nearby.
