Lynmouth

Rare point-break conditions, ability level, parking, facilities and the serious hazards to know before you surf.

Updated 2 August 2026

One of Devon's most respected waves, but strictly for experienced surfers.

Lynmouth is one of Devon's most respected surf spots, but it is completely different from the sandy beaches at Saunton, Woolacombe and Croyde.

It is a rare, rocky point break that requires substantial swell before it begins working. When the conditions align, Lynmouth can produce exceptionally long left-hand waves, but it is strictly for experienced surfers.

What is the surfing like?

Lynmouth breaks over boulders and cobblestones beside the river mouth. Large west or north-westerly swell must wrap into the sheltered coastline, ideally combined with light southerly or south-easterly wind.

When everything aligns, long left-hand waves can peel along the point for several hundred metres. This has earned Lynmouth recognition as one of England's finest point breaks and an important part of the North Devon World Surfing Reserve.

However, Lynmouth spends much of the year flat or unsuitable. It normally requires the sort of powerful winter swell that makes more exposed beaches excessively large or blown out.

Is Lynmouth good for beginners?

No. Lynmouth is not suitable for beginners and should not be treated as a normal surfing beach.

The bottom is rocky, currents can be powerful and entering or leaving the water requires careful timing and local knowledge. There is no regular lifeguard service, surf school or protected beginner area.

Even competent intermediate surfers should watch the break carefully and honestly assess their ability before entering. When Lynmouth is working properly, it is generally best suited to experienced and advanced surfers.

Local visitor guidance also describes Lynmouth as unsuitable for mainstream surfing and bodyboarding because of its steep shingle, rocks and lack of lifeguards.

Parking

The Esplanade Car Park is conveniently positioned in Lynmouth and currently costs £1.60 per hour, up to £16 for the day.

Lower and Upper Lyndale car parks are slightly cheaper at £1.40 per hour, up to £14 for the day. Charges apply throughout the year between 10am and 6pm.

Payment is available through the machines or RingGo. Parking spaces can disappear quickly when Lynmouth is working because local and travelling surfers will all have seen the same forecast.

Facilities and atmosphere

Lynmouth has public toilets, cafes, pubs, shops and accommodation within walking distance of the water.

The setting is spectacular, with wooded cliffs rising behind the harbour and the East Lyn River flowing through the village. It feels more like an Exmoor harbour village than a conventional surf destination.

There is no dedicated surf hire or lesson infrastructure beside the break. Surfers should arrive with all the equipment they need and should not rely on being able to purchase replacements locally.

The drawbacks

Lynmouth's greatest drawback is its lack of reliability. The conditions required are relatively rare, making it unsuitable for planning a normal surf trip far in advance.

The rocky bottom, strong currents and difficult entry and exit present genuine hazards. There is no lifeguard service, and an error can leave a surfer being pushed towards rocks rather than a forgiving sandy beach.

When the wave works, the limited take-off area can also become crowded and competitive. Visitors should respect experienced local surfers and understand standard priority rules.

Surfbooker verdict

Lynmouth is one of Devon's most impressive waves, but it is not a destination for beginners, lessons or casual holiday surfing.

When a large west or north-westerly swell combines with light southerly wind, it can produce exceptionally long and memorable left-hand waves. These conditions are uncommon, and the risks are considerably greater than at Devon's sandy beaches.

Lynmouth deserves its reputation and its place in any complete Devon surf guide, but most surfers should admire it from the shore until they have the necessary experience.
