Wales beach guide

Langland Bay at a glance

Langland Bay sits just west of Mumbles, the first of the sandy bays as you head out along the south Gower from Swansea. A promenade lined with traditional beach huts runs along the back, with cliffs and a golf course behind, and the bay is a designated conservation area. It is a short drive from Swansea along winding roads, with two large car parks behind the beach.

It is a sandy beach with rock pools, rocky in places, and at high tide it splits from its smaller neighbour, Rotherslade. Facilities are good: accessible toilets, outdoor showers, a first aid point, a lost child centre, a shop, a café and the brasserie, and even tennis courts for hire. A ramp from the promenade gives easy access to the sand.

As Swansea's local surf beach, Langland is busy whenever there are waves. RNLI lifeguards patrol through the summer, and dogs are kept off the beach from May to September. In 2026 the RNLI patrolled over the Easter holidays and every day from early May to mid-September. Dogs are not allowed on the beach between 1 May and 30 September and must be on a lead on the promenades at Langland and Rotherslade all year. The coast path runs west over the cliffs to Caswell.

Who it suits

  • Improving surfers
  • Families wanting lifeguards and facilities
  • Wheelchair users, thanks to the promenade and ramp
  • Walkers on the coast path to Caswell

Think twice if…

  • Quiet surf sessions on good days
  • Dog walkers between May and September

Surf guide

Surfing Langland Bay

Langland picks up south-westerly swell and has several different waves across the bay, from sandy peaks to reef sections over the rocky parts. Surfers value it for that variety, and on a good day it draws much of Swansea's surf community.

That means a busy line-up. Beginners are best on the inside on small days, inside the lifeguarded area, leaving the reef sections to experienced surfers who know them.

Level

Improvers and locals

Learners can use the inside on small days; the reefs are for experienced surfers.

Etiquette

Busy local break

Respect priority and don't drop in — it is a community spot.

Hazards

Rocks and crowds

The bay is rocky in places, and the high tide cuts it in two. Use the flags in season.

FAQs

Langland Bay: quick answers

Are there lifeguards at Langland?

Yes. In 2026 the RNLI patrolled over the Easter school holidays and daily from 2 May to 13 September, 10am–6pm.

Can I take my dog to Langland Bay?

Not between 1 May and 30 September. Outside those dates dogs can go on the beach but must be on a lead on the promenade.

Is Langland Bay accessible?

The promenade gives good wheelchair access and a ramp leads onto the sand, and there are accessible toilets.

Is Langland good for beginners?

On small days, on the inside and between the flags. It gets crowded when the surf is good.
