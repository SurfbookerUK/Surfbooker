Woolacombe at a glance

A huge surf beach with plenty of room to find your own peak.

Woolacombe is one of North Devon's best-known surf destinations. The broad sandy beach stretches for around three miles towards Putsborough, with Atlantic swell producing peak after peak along the bay.

That exposure makes Woolacombe versatile. Smaller summer conditions can suit learners, longboarders and families, while winter swells can turn the same beach into a powerful, demanding surf spot. Onshore wind is one of its biggest weaknesses.

The village is geared heavily towards visitors, with plenty of places to eat and drink, surf facilities, shops and family entertainment close to the beach. In peak summer that convenience comes with a downside: it can get very busy.

Surfbooker's take

Woolacombe isn't about one magic take-off spot. Its strength is the amount of beach available. If one section is packed, walking further along the sand can be a much better move than squeezing into the crowd.

Surfbooker local tips

The things we'd tell a mate.

Don't automatically join the crowd

The beach is enormous and there are often multiple peaks. If the main access area is chaotic, take a walk and look for space.

Watch for loose boards

Busy summer days put a lot of inexperienced surfers in the water. Give learners and surf-school groups room and watch for ditched boards when paddling out.

If the wind ruins it, check Putsborough

The southern end sits beneath Baggy Point and can offer more shelter when southerly or south-westerly winds make Woolacombe proper messy.

Self-catering? Try Ilfracombe

Ilfracombe is worth the drive for a wider choice of shops and generally more budget-friendly places to stock up and eat. The Admiral Collingwood Wetherspoons is on the seafront, on the site of the old Collingwood Hotel.

Marine Drive is useful for surfers

If you don't need to be beside the village, Marine Drive runs behind the dunes and can be practical for accessing different parts of the beach.

Stay for sunset

The west-facing bay can produce brilliant sunsets. Hanging around after the surf can be well worth it.

Beach & surf guide

Know Woolacombe before you paddle out.

Open beach, multiple peaks and plenty of room

Woolacombe is predominantly a sandy beach break, so there isn't one fixed line-up that defines the place. Banks move and the best peak changes with tide, swell and season.

On manageable days the width of the bay is a real advantage. Longboards work well, but there is enough punch for shorter boards when the banks and swell line up. Bigger winter surf means harder paddles, heavier waves and stronger water movement.

Summer

Often accessible

Smaller Atlantic swells and forgiving white water can make Woolacombe a strong learner and improver option.

Winter

Can get seriously heavy

Large Atlantic swells can produce powerful sets and demanding paddle-outs.

Wind

Onshore-sensitive

The exposed beach can deteriorate quickly in onshore wind. Cleaner easterly winds can transform it.

Bottom

Mostly sand

The main beach is predominantly sandy, unlike rockier neighbouring sections around Combesgate.

Crowds

Walk for space

Peak summer can be extremely busy around obvious access points, but the beach gives you options.

Safety

Conditions still matter

Rips and lateral movement can develop as surf size increases. Follow lifeguard advice and assess conditions before entering.

Parking

Several options depending on where you want to hit the beach.

Prices vary seasonally, so check current signage rather than relying on an old tariff.

Plan the drive as well as the surf

Woolacombe's coastal setting is part of the appeal, but summer traffic can make the final approach slow. Arriving early gives you a better choice of parking and more time to look along the beach before committing to a peak.

Closest

Among the closest main parking options to the beach and village access.

Surf option

Marine Drive

Runs behind the dunes and is useful when you want the wider beach rather than the busiest village end.

Overnight

No overnight parking

The main beach car parks prohibit overnight parking. Check the exact rules where you park.

Dogs

Dogs on the beach.

Woolacombe is dog-friendly all year because part of the beach remains available to dogs throughout the year. From Good Friday or 1 April, whichever comes first, until 30 September, daytime restrictions operate between 9am and 6pm.

Zone A near the village end is no dogs, Zone B allows dogs on leads, and Zone C south of Mill Rock remains available without the beach-specific seasonal restriction.

It is still a very good beach for visitors with dogs because the unrestricted section is large.

Check the zones

Beach dog restrictions can change. Check the signs at the beach when you arrive, particularly around the start and end of the summer season.

Worth the short trip

Nearby surf spots worth knowing.

You're in the middle of a brilliant stretch of coast

The coast path and short drives connect Woolacombe with Mortehoe, Combesgate, Putsborough, Croyde and Saunton, so changing conditions do not have to mean giving up on the day.

Putsboroughsouthern end of the bay

More sheltered beneath Baggy Point and often worth checking when south or south-westerly winds are spoiling Woolacombe.

Best for: looking for cleaner conditions in troublesome winds.

Combesgatenorth of Woolacombe

A smaller neighbouring option with reef and rock influence. More care is needed around the bottom.

Best for: more experienced surfers looking beyond the main sandy beach.

Croydeshort drive south

A more compact and often more powerful beach break.

Best for: intermediate and advanced surfers when conditions suit.

Saunton Sandssouth beyond Croyde

A huge sandy beach known for slower rolling waves and longboarding.

Best for: longboards and learners on suitable days.

FAQs

Quick answers before you go.

Is Woolacombe good for beginner surfers?

Yes, when conditions are suitable. The sandy bottom, long beach and reliable white water make it popular for learning, but larger surf can make it unsuitable for beginners.

How long is Woolacombe Beach?

The main sandy bay is generally described as around three miles long, extending towards Putsborough.

Does Woolacombe get crowded?

Yes, especially around the main village access in peak summer. Walking away from the busiest area can give you much more room.

Where should I surf if Woolacombe is blown out?

Putsborough is often worth checking when southerly or south-westerly winds are affecting the exposed main beach.

Are dogs allowed?

Yes, year-round, but seasonal zoning creates dog-free and leads-required sections near the village end.

Can I hire surf equipment?

Yes. Surfboards, wetsuits and bodyboards are available from local surf businesses.

Is Woolacombe lifeguarded?

Woolacombe has seasonal lifeguard cover. Check current patrol dates and follow the beach flags and lifeguard instructions.

Is Woolacombe good for families?

Yes. The huge beach plus food, shops, arcades, cinema and other activities make it practical when only part of the family wants to surf.
