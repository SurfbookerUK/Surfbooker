Cornwall beach guide

Praa Sands at a glance

Praa Sands (pronounced 'pray') is about a mile of fine, shell-based sand on Cornwall's south coast, just off the A394 between Penzance and Helston. It faces south into Mount's Bay, sits inside the Cornwall National Landscape, and is backed by grass-topped dunes with headlands at each end. Because it faces the opposite way to the north coast, it's the beach many surfers head for when northerly winds have blown the north-coast breaks out.

Most people arrive at the western end, where the car parks, a café, a general store and a slipway give an easy, level route onto the sand. Walk east towards Hendra and it gets noticeably quieter. Erosion has exposed a thin layer of peat around 2,000 years old that was once buried under the dunes, which is why the beach is listed as a regionally important geological site. A wartime pillbox has slipped from the cliff onto the sand.

There's history behind the beach too. A memorial on the green remembers the damaged Sunderland flying boat beached here in 1943, when villagers helped its crew ashore. The South West Coast Path runs along the back of the beach, east to Rinsey Head and west towards Marazion and St Michael's Mount, so it's easy to mix a surf with a walk. Basking sharks, seals and dolphins are occasionally seen offshore.

Who it suits

  • Surfers looking for a south-coast option when the north is blown out
  • Lessons on small, clean days
  • Families wanting facilities close to the sand at the western end
  • Coast-path walkers heading to Rinsey Head

Think twice if…

  • Dog walkers on the beach from 10am to 6pm in July and August
  • Weak swimmers at high tide, when the beach shelves steeply
  • Surfers expecting north-coast consistency on a small swell

Surf guide

Surfing Praa Sands

Praa faces south, so it needs swell that makes its way into Mount's Bay, which gives it a different swell window from the north coast. When it does get swell, the waves can be powerful and are a test even for experienced surfers. It's often at its best around high tide, but that's also when the beach shelves fastest and the shorebreak can dump hard.

Light northerly winds are the ones to look for. The wind here can be very local and gusty, so it may be blowing at Praa when it's calm a few miles away. There are rocks near both headlands and the beach is known for strong undercurrents, so stay between the flags when lifeguards are on duty.

Level

Small days for learners

Lessons run on suitable days; bigger swells bring powerful waves best left to experienced surfers.

Wind

Light northerly

Offshore here, and often the reason to come when the north coast is messy.

Hazards

High-tide shorebreak

Steeply shelving sand, a heavy shore dump at high tide, undercurrents and rocks by the headlands.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Western end car parks

The car parks are at the western end, with a level route to the sand via the slipway. The eastern end at Hendra is a longer walk.

Facilities

Clustered at the western end

Toilets are near the car parks and beach entrance, with a café overlooking the sand, a general store and a surf shop nearby.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

The RNLI patrols the main beach in season. No flags means no lifeguards; check this year's dates on the RNLI website.

Dogs

Peak-summer daytime ban

Cornwall Council prohibits dogs on Praa Sands from 10am to 6pm, 1 July to 31 August. They're welcome at other times.

Getting there

A394

About ten minutes' drive from Penzance towards Helston on the A394. Penzance is the nearest main railway station.

FAQs

Praa Sands: quick answers

How do you pronounce Praa Sands?

'Pray Sands'.

When can dogs go on Praa Sands?

All year except 10am to 6pm from 1 July to 31 August.

Is Praa Sands good when the north coast is flat?

Not necessarily. Praa needs swell to reach Mount's Bay, so it's at its best when there's swell about but northerly winds have spoiled the north coast.

Is it safe to swim at high tide?

Take care. The sand shelves quickly at high tide and the shorebreak can be heavy, which is a particular risk for inexperienced swimmers. Swim between the red and yellow flags when lifeguards are on duty.
