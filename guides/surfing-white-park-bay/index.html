White Park Bay at a glance

White Park Bay is a white arc of sand between two chalk headlands on the North Antrim coast, backed by ancient dunes and cared for by the National Trust since 1938. It lies off the B146, 6.5 miles from Bushmills and 7.5 miles from Ballycastle with the hamlet of Portbraddan at its western end. The National Trust car park is open all day, and the bay itself is open from dawn to dusk.

It's a place for a walk rather than a swim. The National Trust warns that swimming is unsafe because of dangerous rip currents, and there are no lifeguards, toilets or facilities. Getting down involves steep steps, slopes and kissing gates, and the small free car park has a rough surface. Cattle and sheep graze the dunes and beach, so dogs must stay on leads at all times. NCN route 93 passes nearby for cyclists.

Look out for fossils in the chalk cliffs and on the beach, including fragments of belemnites, squid-like creatures from the late Cretaceous. There is a scheduled historic cairn above the beach. Avoid the unstable cliff between the bay and Portbraddan after a rockfall there, and only walk across to Portbraddan along the beach at low tide. A National Trust holiday cottage sits at Portbraddan.

Surf guide

Surf and safety

Forecast sites describe White Park Bay as a fairly exposed beach break that works best on a north-westerly swell with south-easterly offshore winds. That doesn't make it a sensible surf beach for most people: it is remote, unpatrolled and known for strong rips.

Any surfing here is only for experienced surfers who know the area and are with others. For lessons or a lifeguarded session, head to Whiterocks, Portrush or Benone.

FAQs

White Park Bay: quick answers

Can you swim at White Park Bay?

The National Trust says swimming is unsafe because of dangerous rip currents. There are no lifeguards.

Are there toilets?

No.

Can I walk to Portbraddan?

Only along the beach at low tide, and keep away from the unstable cliff.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, on a lead, because livestock graze the bay.
