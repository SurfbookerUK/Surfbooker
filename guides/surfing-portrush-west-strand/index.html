Northern Ireland beach guide

Portrush West Strand at a glance

West Strand — also known as West Bay or Mill Strand — sits right in the middle of Portrush, with the harbour at its northern end and a pedestrian and cycle promenade running along its back. You can walk down from the town centre in minutes, which makes it the easiest beach on the coast to combine with lunch, a wander round the shops or an evening out. It holds Blue Flag status, most recently in 2024.

The beach has a story under the sand. The council notes it is protected as an Area of Special Scientific Interest because of buried peat beds and ancient sands, which hold fossil remains of alder and birch trees from roughly 9,000 to 7,000 years ago. Keep watching the water too: a resident pod of harbour porpoises is often seen just offshore.

For surfers it is a handy alternative to East Strand, facing a slightly different direction, so it is always worth comparing the two before you change. Lifeguards patrol in July and August. Facilities are mostly in town rather than on the sand, and the beach is narrower than its eastern neighbour, so it can feel cramped on a busy high tide in summer.

Who it suits

  • Families who want town facilities on the doorstep
  • Lessons and beginners on small days
  • Surfers checking more than one Portrush beach
  • Car-free visitors arriving by train

Think twice if…

  • Wide-open space at high tide
  • Long walks — East Strand or Whiterocks suit those better

Surf guide

Surfing Portrush West Strand

West Strand is a sandy beach break with lefts and rights. Like the rest of the Portrush coast it relies on north-westerly swell, and summer is often small or flat. Because it faces a different way from East Strand, one beach can be cleaner than the other on the same day, so check both before you commit.

The harbour wall at the northern end, swimmers close to the promenade and a narrowing beach at high tide all need care. Stay inside the black-and-white flagged zone for boards when lifeguards are on duty, and keep clear of harbour traffic.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

Seasonal charges

Parking is charged from 1 April to 30 September. Accessible parking and cycle parking are available.

Facilities

Town on the doorstep

Accessible toilets, plus cafés, restaurants, shops and amusements behind the promenade.

Lifeguards

Seasonal RNLI patrol

The council lists a seasonal RNLI patrol, 10am–6pm daily in July and August. Check the RNLI site for this year's dates.

Dogs

Summer restrictions

The council allows dogs but applies restrictions from 1 June to 15 September — check the signs at the entrance for where they apply.

Portrush railway station is a short walk away through the town.

FAQs

Portrush West Strand: quick answers

Is West Strand the same as West Bay?

Yes. It is also called West Bay or Mill Strand.

Are there lifeguards?

The council lists a seasonal RNLI patrol, 10am–6pm daily in July and August.

Can I see porpoises?

The council says a resident pod of harbour porpoises can be seen just off the shore.

Is parking charged?

Yes, from 1 April to 30 September.
