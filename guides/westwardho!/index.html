Beach & surf guide

Westward Ho! beach guide

Westward Ho! sits on the North Devon coast beside Bideford Bay. Its broad sandy beach, village facilities and established surf scene make it one of the area's most practical places for a first surf lesson, a family beach day or a relaxed weekend by the coast.

At low tide the sand opens up dramatically, giving surfers, walkers and families a huge amount of room. Around the village you'll find surf schools, surf shops, cafes, pubs, restaurants and other useful facilities within easy reach of the beach.

Beyond the main seafront, Northam Burrows gives the destination a very different feel: open grassland, dunes, wildlife and access to a quieter end of the beach.

Before you go

Check the tide and current sea conditions before you set off. Westward Ho! changes dramatically through the tide, especially around the slipway, rock pools and pebble ridge.

The slipway end

This is the easiest place to start if you want the village close by. The main slipway gives straightforward access to the sand, with cafes, shops, amusements, toilets and other facilities close to the seafront.

For first-time visitors, families and anyone who wants the practical side of the day to be simple, this is usually the obvious starting point.

Surf

Beginner friendly

A sandy bottom and plentiful white water on suitable days make Westward Ho! a practical place to learn. Conditions still vary, so choose the day carefully.

Tides

Check before you travel

The beach changes enormously through the tide. Low tide exposes a huge area of sand and rock pools; high tide pushes the water back towards the pebble ridge.

Safety

Use lifeguarded areas

When lifeguards are operating, follow the flags and local advice. Conditions and safe entry or exit points can change through the tide.

Surfbooker local tips

The things we'd tell a mate.

Surfing? Head towards Northam Burrows

For an early surf, the Northam Burrows end is worth considering. Arriving before seasonal toll charging starts can mean free parking and it gives quick access to the quieter northern end of the beach. Bear in mind that the final beach access is more uneven than the main slipway because you need to cross the Pebble Ridge. Check the current gate, access and toll times before relying on this.

Lunch after your surf

Grab lunch from Atlantic Bay Fish & Chips after your session. They also sell cider, and the large grass area by the surf shops is an easy place to sit down, eat and let the wetsuit dry off a bit.

Couples and surfers: consider Northam

If the beach is the priority but you don't need to stay in the middle of the resort, Northam is another option. It's quieter and less touristy, with easy access towards Northam Burrows. Durrant House Hotel is roughly a five-minute drive from Westward Ho! and is often one of the more cost-effective hotel options aside from Airbnb. It's an older, more no-frills hotel and can feel a little rough around the edges, but it can be good value if price and location matter more than polish.

With kids: stay in Westward Ho! itself

If you're travelling with children, staying in Westward Ho! itself is hard to beat. The beach, rock pools, arcades, cafes and shops are all close together, so you can do most of the day on foot without repeatedly loading everyone back into the car.

Stay for a sea sunset

On clear evenings, roughly late April through early August is the period to look for the classic sun-dropping-into-the-sea view. The beach, promenade and Pebble Ridge all work well, so don't automatically leave straight after your surf.

Travelling with a dog?

Dogs are permitted on part of the beach all year. From 1 May to 30 September, dogs are prohibited south of Sandymere Bridge, while dogs remain permitted on the northern beach between Sandymere Bridge and the Taw/Torridge estuary. During the restriction period, dogs must be kept on leads on the promenade.

Parking & facilities

Know before you go

Westward Ho! has multiple parking options rather than relying on a single beach car park. Always check current signs, charges and access conditions when you arrive.

Village: Main parking

Useful for the seafront, shops, food and the main slipway. A good default if you're visiting the centre of Westward Ho!.

Beach access: Slipway area

Convenient for getting onto the beach, particularly if carrying kit or visiting with someone who benefits from easier access.

Quieter end: Northam Burrows

Seasonal vehicle access can take you towards the quieter northern end of the beach. A toll may apply during the main season, so check current information first. Access onto the beach is more uneven here than at the main slipway because you need to cross the Pebble Ridge, so it is less suitable for pushchairs, wheelchairs or anyone with limited mobility.

Facilities: Toilets & showers

Facilities are available around the village end, with additional facilities around the Northam Burrows visitor area.

Accessibility: Slipway access

The concrete slipway provides one of the easiest routes onto the sand. Accessible toilets and designated parking are available in the area.

Dogs: Seasonal restrictions

Dogs are permitted on part of the beach all year. From 1 May to 30 September, dogs are prohibited south of Sandymere Bridge, while the northern beach between Sandymere Bridge and the Taw/Torridge estuary remains available. Dogs must be kept on leads on the promenade during the restriction period.

Did you know?

Westward Ho! is genuinely unusual.

There is more here than surf and sand, and a few details are worth remembering.

The exclamation mark

Westward Ho! is the only place in the UK with an exclamation mark in its official name.

Named after a novel

The village took its name from Charles Kingsley's 1855 novel Westward Ho!.

A Victorian resort

Tourism investment followed the book's success and helped create the seaside resort.

Ancient forest

Remains of a submerged prehistoric forest have been uncovered on the beach.

Northam Burrows

The neighbouring dunes, grassland and salt marsh form an important coastal habitat.

Estuary waterfronts around North Devon

For a calmer afternoon away from the main seaside resort, explore the estuary waterfronts. The estuary waterfront is great for an easy wander, food and a drink, and the sunsets can be excellent.

Johns is a good breakfast stop, and there are prominent waterfront restaurant options for anything from a relaxed meal to more of an occasion. The estuary waterfront is also very popular with dog walkers, but dog access rules can vary by season and beach section, so check the current signs before setting off.

Best for: couples, breakfast, dog walks, waterfront food and sunsets.

Appledoreabout a 10-minute drive

A small, characterful harbour village that's ideal when you want a few hours away from the beach. Wander the narrow streets, browse independent and boutique-style shops, stop at one of the cafes and finish along the harbour.

Best for: independent shops, cafes, harbour walks and a slower afternoon.

FAQs

Quick answers before you go.

Is Westward Ho! good for beginner surfers?

Yes. Its sandy bottom, long beach and choice of established surf schools make it one of North Devon's most practical places for a first lesson. Conditions still matter, so beginners should choose suitable days or book with a local school.

How many main surf schools are there in Westward Ho!?

Surfbooker currently lists three main schools serving Westward Ho!: Surf SUP Academy, North Devon Surf School and Westward Waves Surf School.

Are there surf shops in Westward Ho!?

Yes. Ho! Surf and SOS Surf are the two local surf shops Surfbooker is currently including in this destination guide.

Can dogs go on Westward Ho! Beach?

Yes. Dogs are permitted on part of the beach all year. From 1 May to 30 September, dogs are prohibited south of Sandymere Bridge, while the northern beach between Sandymere Bridge and the Taw/Torridge estuary remains available. Dogs must be kept on leads on the promenade during the restriction period.

Does Westward Ho! have lifeguards?

RNLI lifeguard cover operates seasonally. Always check current patrol dates and use the designated lifeguarded areas when they are in operation.

What happens to the beach at high tide?

The large sandy area reduces substantially as the tide comes in, and access to the water can involve the pebble ridge in places. Check tide times before planning a long beach day.

Why does Westward Ho! have an exclamation mark?

The resort was named after Charles Kingsley's 1855 novel Westward Ho!. The punctuation came with the title and remains part of the official place name.

When is the best time for a sunset over the sea?

For the classic Westward Ho! sea sunset, roughly late April through early August is the period to look at. Exact sunset position changes through the year, and cloud can obviously make or break the view.

Is there anything to do besides surfing?

Yes. The main alternatives are Northam Burrows, the sea pool and rock pools, golf, amusements and coastal walks. See the Things to do section above for the short version.
