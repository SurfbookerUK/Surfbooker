Southern England beach guide

Compton Bay at a glance

Compton Bay runs along the south-west coast of the Isle of Wight between Freshwater Bay and Brook, below the chalk ridge of Compton Down. The National Trust looks after the bay, the downs and three car parks along the Military Road. The shore slopes gently, the sea stays shallow a long way out, and the soft cliffs behind are constantly crumbling onto the sand.

It's famous for fossils as much as surf. The rocks here date from the Cretaceous, around 130 million years ago, and the beach turns up dinosaur bones, footprint casts near Hanover Point and fossilised wood. The National Trust runs guided fossil walks on set dates, and the Isle of Wight Coast Path and Round the Island cycle route both pass along the top.

For surfers it's the island's most reliable beach, facing into the prevailing south-westerly swell with lefts and rights along the bay and at Hanover Point. It works best around high tide, and while summer is often flat, winter can bring powerful days. There are no lifeguards, and the National Trust warns that surf and currents can be strong depending on the weather.

Surf guide

Surfing Compton Bay

Beach breaks with several peaks, including 'Fields' and Hanover Point, offering both lefts and rights. A south-westerly swell with north-easterly winds is the classic combination, and it's usually best around high tide.

Summer is mostly flat; February is statistically the best month. The cliffs are eroding, so don't sit or change directly beneath them, and remember there's no lifeguard cover.

Level

All levels

On smaller days; it can get powerful.

Hazards

Currents and cliffs

Strong surf and currents, and eroding cliffs — don't sit below them.

Season

Autumn and winter

Channel swell is most likely from autumn to early spring; summer is usually small and much busier.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

National Trust car parks

Brook, Hanover and Compton Chine car parks, paid via the JustPark app; free for registered NT members. No overnight or coach parking.

Facilities

Hanover Point

Toilets, baby change, an accessible toilet and a Changing Places cubicle. A seasonal catering van sells food and drink.

Access

Wooden steps

Steps with handrails at Compton Bay car park and Brook, and a longer flight near Compton Chine. Downland paths are steep.

Dogs

Seasonal

Dogs welcome all year between Hanover Point and Brook Chine; banned west of Compton Bay car park 15 May–15 September.

Getting here

Summer bus

Southern Vectis runs from Newport to Compton Bay in summer only.

FAQs

Compton Bay: quick answers

Is Compton Bay good for surfing?

It's the Isle of Wight's most consistent surf beach, facing the south-westerly swell, and usually best around high tide.

How do you pay for parking?

Through the JustPark app; National Trust members park free once registered.

Can dogs go on Compton Bay?

Yes, between Hanover Point and Brook Chine all year. West of the Compton car park, dogs are banned from 15 May to 15 September.

Where are the dinosaur footprints?

Casts are found on the beach near Hanover Point; the NT runs guided fossil walks that must be booked.
