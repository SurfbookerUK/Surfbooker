Northeast England & Yorkshire beach guide

Bamburgh at a glance

Bamburgh's beach runs for miles beneath the castle, a broad sweep of pale sand with the Bamburgh Dunes behind it and the Farne Islands out to sea. The dunes are a Site of Special Scientific Interest and the whole stretch lies within the Northumberland Coast Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, so it feels far wilder than its fame suggests.

The castle on its rock has a long story: the site was an Anglo-Saxon fort before the Normans built on it, and it's first mentioned in writing in 547. In the village, St Aidan's church stands where Aidan built a wooden church in 635, and Grace Darling, the Victorian lifeboat heroine, is buried in its churchyard, with a museum opposite.

For surfers, Bamburgh is one of the most consistent and scenic breaks on this coast. The sand slopes gently, which suits a wide range of abilities, and surf schools meet at the North Car Park in spring, summer and autumn. There are no lifeguards, though, so come prepared.

Who it suits

  • Mixed-ability groups on smaller days
  • Scenic surf trips with castle views
  • Learners with a seasonal school
  • Dog walkers year-round

Think twice if…

  • Anyone who needs lifeguard cover
  • Big-town facilities on the beach

Surf guide

Surfing Bamburgh

Bamburgh faces the North Sea and needs swell from the north or east to work. A rocky outcrop at the north end of the beach produces good left-handers on a northerly swell, while the main stretch offers sandy peaks with a gradual slope. Westerly winds blow offshore.

Bamburgh's local surfing guide warns that rip currents form in bigger surf. With no lifeguards, it's worth knowing the RNLI's rip-current advice before you go, and if you or anyone else gets into difficulty, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.

Level

All levels

The gentle sandy slope suits learners on small days; the north-end left suits better surfers.

Hazards

Rips in bigger surf

No lifeguards. Call 999 and ask for the Coastguard in an emergency.

Season

Autumn to spring

North Sea swell is most reliable outside summer; summer is often small but good for lessons.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

North Car Park

On The Wynding, the main meeting point for surf schools, with a short walk through the dunes to the beach.

Lifeguards

None

Bamburgh isn't RNLI-lifeguarded.

Dogs

Year-round

Northumberland's beaches have no seasonal dog ban. Keep dogs under control near nesting birds in the dunes.

Facilities

In the village

Toilets, cafés and pubs are in Bamburgh village rather than on the beach.

Wildlife

Protected dunes

The Bamburgh Dunes are a Site of Special Scientific Interest, so stick to the paths through them.

FAQs

Bamburgh: quick answers

Is Bamburgh good for surfing?

Yes. It's one of Northumberland's most consistent breaks, with a gentle sandy slope and lefts off the rocks at the north end on a northerly swell.

Is Bamburgh beach lifeguarded?

No. Call 999 and ask for the Coastguard if anyone gets into difficulty.

Where do surfers park at Bamburgh?

The North Car Park on The Wynding, a short walk through the dunes.

Can I take my dog on Bamburgh beach?

Yes, year-round. Keep it under control near nesting birds.
