Scotland beach guide

Fraserburgh at a glance

Fraserburgh beach curves for around three miles east of the town towards the Waters of Philorth local nature reserve. It's a town beach in the true sense: the harbour, shops and car parks are right behind it, so it's an easy place to surf without a long walk in.

At the town end stands Kinnaird Head, where a castle built from 1570 by Sir Alexander Fraser was converted into Scotland's first mainland lighthouse, first lit in 1787. The original lighthouse is now part of the Museum of Scottish Lighthouses, with lenses from lights all around the Scottish coast.

For surfing there are two different breaks. The open beach has lefts and rights spread along the sand, while a left-hand point works off the harbour end over rock. Surf-forecast calls the spot fairly consistent and usually uncrowded, though it's exposed and takes the full force of the weather.

Who it suits

  • Learners and intermediates on the open beach
  • Experienced surfers looking for the harbour-end left
  • Surf trips that combine waves with a town and a museum

Think twice if…

  • Beginners near the harbour rocks
  • Anyone needing lifeguard cover

Surf guide

Surfing Fraserburgh

Surf-forecast gives a northerly swell as best, with light southerly winds offshore. It works through all stages of the tide on the beach, and takes both groundswell and wind swell. The harbour-end point is more sensitive to the tide and the rocks, so watch it before paddling out.

Winter and spring are the strongest seasons, with June the most often clean month. The guide's warning is short: take care of rips and rocks.

FAQs

Fraserburgh: quick answers

Which conditions suit Fraserburgh?

A northerly swell with light southerly winds. The beach works on all tides.

Is Fraserburgh crowded?

Surf-forecast describes it as usually uncrowded, even when there are waves.

Is parking free?

Aberdeenshire Council lists Seaforth Street South (144 spaces) as free.

What is there to see nearby?

The Museum of Scottish Lighthouses at Kinnaird Head, where Scotland's first mainland lighthouse was lit in 1787.
