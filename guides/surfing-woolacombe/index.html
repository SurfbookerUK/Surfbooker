Woolacombe

Waves, tides, beginner suitability, parking, facilities and the drawbacks to know before you visit.

Updated 2 August 2026

One of Devon's best all-round surf beaches.

Woolacombe is one of Devon's most popular surfing destinations. It combines a huge sandy beach with reliable Atlantic swell, plenty of facilities and a lively village immediately behind it.

It works well for beginners, improving surfers and more experienced riders, but its popularity means traffic, parking and crowded summer line-ups can become frustrating.

What is the surfing like?

Woolacombe is a long sandy beach break with numerous left- and right-breaking peaks. The amount of space means you can usually walk away from the busiest area and find a quieter section.

It is more versatile than Saunton, with enough whitewater for beginners but also faster, more powerful waves when the swell increases. Woolacombe works through the tides, although the position and quality of the peaks change throughout the day.

Easterly winds produce the cleanest conditions. If the main beach is messy in a south-westerly wind, the Putsborough end can offer greater shelter.

Is Woolacombe good for beginners?

Yes. Woolacombe is a very good place to learn when the waves are small and manageable. The sandy bottom, consistent whitewater and large beach create a forgiving learning environment.

There are several established surf schools, including a Surfing England-accredited Centre of Excellence. Woolacombe Surf Centre

However, Woolacombe can become powerful when the swell increases and rip currents can develop. Surfers should use the black-and-white flagged surfing area, while swimmers and bodyboarders should remain between the red-and-yellow flags.

The beach receives extensive seasonal RNLI lifeguard coverage, but visitors should always check whether flags are present. Woolacombe RNLI information

Parking

Woolacombe has several parking options, but the village and approach roads can become extremely busy during sunny weekends and school holidays.

The Esplanade and Sandy Burrows car parks are closest to the beach, with daily charges generally varying between £6 and £14 depending on the season.

Marine Drive runs behind the dunes and is useful for accessing quieter parts of the beach. Parking costs approximately £6 in winter, £8 during the middle season and £10 during peak periods. National Trust members can park there free of charge.

Arriving early or after 4pm can reduce both the cost and the stress. Check current Woolacombe parking information

Facilities and atmosphere

Woolacombe has more facilities than most Devon surf beaches. The village has cafes, pubs, restaurants, surf shops, equipment hire, lessons, toilets and accommodation within walking distance of the beach.

The atmosphere is lively, family-friendly and strongly connected to surfing. It is an excellent destination if some members of the group want to surf while others prefer shops, food or a day on the beach.

It feels busier and more commercial than Saunton or Westward Ho!, particularly during the summer, but everything you need is conveniently close.

The drawbacks

Traffic into Woolacombe can be slow during peak periods and the main car parks are relatively expensive.

The village end of the beach can become crowded with surf schools, beginners, bodyboarders and experienced surfers sharing the same general area. Walking further along the beach usually improves matters, although this means carrying your equipment further.

The main beach is also exposed to the prevailing south-westerly wind, which can quickly make the waves messy.

Surfbooker verdict

Woolacombe is one of Devon's best all-round surfing destinations. It offers reliable waves for different ability levels, extensive facilities and enough beach to escape much of the crowd if you are prepared to walk.

Saunton may be better for relaxed longboarding and gentler progression, while Croyde offers more powerful waves for experienced surfers. Woolacombe sits comfortably between the two and provides the best overall option for groups and families with mixed requirements.

The traffic, parking costs and summer crowds are its main disadvantages, but as a complete surf destination, Woolacombe is difficult to beat.
