Northern Ireland beach guide

Portstewart Strand at a glance

Portstewart Strand is two miles of National Trust sand, known locally simply as The Strand, running from the edge of Portstewart towards the mouth of the River Bann. It is backed by dunes around 6,000 years old, protected as an Area of Special Scientific Interest, and from the beach you can see Mussenden Temple on the cliffs across the river.

Its quirk is that you can drive onto managed parts of the beach and park on the sand, which makes unloading boards and family kit very easy. Spaces are limited, payment is by contactless card only, and every car must leave by the time shown at the entrance. There are toilets, a Changing Places facility, blue-badge parking and beach wheelchairs to book through the Mae Murray Foundation.

The strand is long enough to find space even on a busy day, and the 3.5-mile Dune and Estuary Walk loops through the dunes to the Bann. Mobile signal can be patchy, so sort out plans before you arrive. Bus 140 runs from Coleraine station to Strand Corner. Remember the National Trust zones the beach, with rules on where boards and dogs can go, and asks you not to dig into the dune faces, which can collapse.

Who it suits

Surf guide

Surfing Portstewart Strand

Portstewart Strand is an exposed beach break with lefts and rights spread along its length. Forecast sites rate it best on a north-westerly swell with offshore winds from the south-east, and it is surfable through the tide. Like the rest of this coast it is often flat in summer and can get crowded when it is working.

The National Trust allows water craft only beyond marker post 10 in June, July and August, and uses the black-and-white chequered flag zone for surfboards when lifeguards are on duty. Swim and bodyboard between the red and yellow flags, and stay out of the water if a red flag is flying.

Parking, facilities & safety

Know before you go

Parking

On the beach

Limited spaces, contactless card only; free for National Trust members and blue-badge holders. Last vehicle entry is an hour before closing.

Facilities

Inclusive beach

Toilets, a Changing Places facility opened with a RADAR key, blue-badge parking and bookable beach wheelchairs.

Lifeguards

Summer season

The National Trust says lifeguards are on duty in June, July and August — choose the lifeguarded section.

Dogs

Leads near the entrance

Dogs must be on leads except in the dog area beyond marker post 10.

Water quality

Check notices

In October 2026 the National Trust issued advice against bathing after algal scum was found on the tide line. Check the website before you go.

FAQs

Portstewart Strand: quick answers

Can you drive on Portstewart Strand?

Yes, onto managed parts of the beach. Spaces are limited, payment is contactless only, and cars must leave by the time posted at the entrance.

Where can I surf?

The National Trust allows water craft beyond post 10 in June, July and August. Use the black-and-white flag zone when it is set out.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes. Keep dogs on leads, except in the dog area beyond post 10.

How do I get there without a car?

Take the train to Coleraine, then bus 140 to Strand Corner.
