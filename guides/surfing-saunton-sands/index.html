Quick verdict

One of Devon's best beaches for beginners, improvers and longboarders.

Saunton Sands is one of Devon's most recognisable surf beaches. It has miles of sand, rolling waves and a relaxed atmosphere that feels less intense than nearby Croyde.

It is particularly well suited to beginners, improving surfers and longboarders, but there are a few practical drawbacks worth knowing before you set off.

What is the surfing like?

Saunton is known for long, gentler waves that can provide much longer rides than some of Devon's punchier beach breaks.

The sandy bottom and relatively forgiving waves make it a good place to practise catching unbroken waves, turning and building confidence. Longboarders particularly love it, although softboards, funboards and wider shortboards all work well when conditions suit.

The beach is enormous, so walking further away from the main entrance can help you find more space.

Is Saunton good for beginners?

Yes. Saunton is one of Devon's strongest options for beginners and improving surfers. Surfing England describes it as having long, rolling waves suitable for beginner and intermediate surfers.

However, it is still an exposed Atlantic beach. Conditions can become powerful, there can be rip currents and the paddle out becomes tiring when the waves get bigger.

Beginners should stay within the appropriate lifeguarded area or book a lesson when unsure.

Parking

There is a large car park immediately behind the beach, which makes carrying boards and equipment relatively straightforward.

Parking now uses ANPR and is paid before leaving. Current charges include £8.50 for up to four hours, followed by £1 for every additional hour or part of an hour. Paying through the app adds a service charge, so using the on-site payment machines is cheaper.

On sunny days and during school holidays, the car park can fill by around 10:30am. Arriving early or visiting later in the afternoon is often the better option.

Facilities and atmosphere

Saunton has a beach shop, food and drink, surfboard and wetsuit hire, surf lessons and seasonal beach-hut hire.

The atmosphere is relaxed and surf-focused, with impressive dunes behind the beach. It feels more like a complete beach day than somewhere you would combine with walking around a town.

The drawbacks

The parking is relatively expensive and can become extremely busy during good weather.

At low tide, the sea retreats a long way across the gently sloping beach, meaning a substantial walk while carrying your board.

The waves can also feel slow or lack power for experienced shortboarders looking for something more challenging.

Surfbooker verdict

Saunton Sands is one of Devon's best beaches for beginners, improving surfers and longboarders. The waves are generally more forgiving than Croyde, there is plenty of space and everything needed for a surf session is available beside the beach.

The parking cost, summer crowds and long low-tide walk are the main negatives. However, for relaxed surfing, longer rides and building confidence, Saunton is difficult to beat.
