Southern England Surf Hire

Bournemouth

Seven miles of sandy beach between Poole and Hengistbury Head — sheltered, family-friendly and only occasionally surfy, with lifeguards and facilities everywhere.

Boscombe Pier

Bournemouth's surf hub beside Boscombe Pier — small, inconsistent Channel waves, but surf schools, lifeguards and seafront cafés all within a few minutes' walk.

Kimmeridge Bay

A rocky bay on the Jurassic Coast with flat limestone ledges, Clavell Tower above, and reef breaks that work on a big Channel swell — strictly for experienced surfers.

Swanage

A sheltered, east-facing sandy bay below a classic Victorian resort — superb for families and swimming, and only rarely surfable.

Highcliffe

A long shingle-and-sand beach below the cliffs at Highcliffe, with views to the Needles, two large car parks and a café at the top.

Southbourne

A quieter end of Bournemouth's long beach, below Southbourne's cliff-top high street and towards Hengistbury Head — often a little more swell than the town centre.

East Wittering

A shingle-and-sand village beach between West Wittering and Bracklesham, with a surf shop in the village and its best waves in autumn and winter.

West Wittering

A huge, privately managed sandy beach at the mouth of Chichester Harbour — immensely popular in summer, with a watersports centre and surf in autumn and winter.

Bracklesham Bay

A shingle-and-sand bay east of the Witterings — known for fossils and, in autumn and winter, Channel surf.

Shoreham

A long shingle beach and Local Nature Reserve beside the River Adur's harbour mouth — occasional surf, and a lot of nature.

Brighton

An urban shingle seafront where the waves are rare and fickle — but when the Channel delivers, Brighton's surfers are out around the groynes and the old West Pier.

Eastbourne

A sheltered shingle resort beach in the lee of Beachy Head — good for swimming, paddleboarding and kayaking, rarely for surfing.

Joss Bay

A small sandy bay between chalk cliffs near Broadstairs — Kent's best-known surf beach, with a surf school, lifeguards and a café.

Compton Bay

A long, south-west-facing sweep of sand below chalk downs and crumbling cliffs — the Isle of Wight's most consistent surf, with dinosaur footprints on the beach.

Freshwater Bay

A small chalk-cliffed cove with a slipway and a rocky reef that can be hollow and powerful — one for experienced surfers.

Shanklin

A long sandy resort beach on the Isle of Wight's sheltered east coast, with an esplanade, a cliff lift and only occasional small surf.
