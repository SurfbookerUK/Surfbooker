Wales Surf Hire

Llangennith

The north end of Rhossili Bay's three-mile sweep of sand — Gower's go-to surf beach, with a surf village and campsite behind the dunes.

Rhossili Bay

One of Britain's most photographed beaches — three miles of sand beneath Rhossili Down, with Worm's Head at the end and the Atlantic on its doorstep.

Caswell Bay

A compact, sandy bay a few minutes from Swansea, with lifeguards in summer, a surf school, showers and a café open all year.

Langland Bay

Swansea's local surf beach — green-and-white beach huts, a busy line-up and a clifftop path to Caswell.

Oxwich Bay

Two and a half miles of sand and dunes in a sheltered south Gower bay — a family and watersports beach that only surfs in big storms.

Port Eynon

A cheerful village beach on the south Gower, joined to Horton at low tide, with lifeguards in summer and a slipway for boats.

Newgale

Two miles of open sand on St Brides Bay, backed by a huge pebble bank — Pembrokeshire's most exposed and dependable surf beach.

Freshwater West

Wild, dune-backed and fully open to the Atlantic — Wales' most consistent and powerful beach break, and no place for a casual swim.

Whitesands

St Davids' own surf beach — a sandy bay beneath Carn Llidi with a surf school on the sand and the cathedral city ten minutes away.

Broad Haven

A sandy village beach on St Brides Bay with cafés, a pub and a surf shop on the seafront — the easy, family-friendly alternative to Newgale.

Manorbier

A small sand-and-pebble bay guarded by a Norman castle, with surf over sand and reef when a south-westerly swell rolls in.

Freshwater East

A dune-backed bay sheltered by Trewent Point — calm on most days, with occasional waves when the wind and swell turn east or south.

Marloes Sands

A wild National Trust beach of sand and rock fins, reached on foot and best enjoyed at low tide with the islands on the horizon.

Nolton Haven

A small, sheltered cove between Newgale and Broad Haven, with a pub, a slipway and dogs welcome all year.

Rhosneigr

Anglesey's watersports village — two west-facing beaches, wind and swell from the Irish Sea, and cafés a short walk from the sand.

Hell's Mouth (Porth Neigwl)

Four miles of open sand on the Llŷn Peninsula, facing straight into the south-west swell — North Wales' most consistent surf beach.

Rest Bay, Porthcawl

South Wales' most accessible surf beach — west-facing sand beside a watersports centre with showers, changing, hire, lessons and a café.
